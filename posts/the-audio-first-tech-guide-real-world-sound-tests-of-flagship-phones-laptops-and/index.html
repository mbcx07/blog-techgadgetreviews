The Audio-First Tech Guide: Real-World Sound Tests of Flagship Phones, Laptops, and Tablets

2026-06-17 · Audio · Tags: audio, techgadgetreviews, The

When we talk about flagship smartphones, premium laptops, and high-end tablets, the conversation usually revolves around processors, camera megapixels, and display refresh rates. But there's a silent hero that often gets overlooked: audio. Whether you're streaming your favorite playlist, watching a movie on a long flight, or taking a critical conference call, the quality of sound can make or break the experience. As a tech reviewer who has spent countless hours with the latest devices, I've come to realize that specs on paper don't always translate to real-world performance. That's why I've put the current generation of top-tier phones, laptops, and tablets through rigorous audio testing—listening to music, watching films, and making calls—to see which ones truly deliver. In this guide, I'll break down the audio strengths and weaknesses of the iPhone 16 Pro Max, Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra, Google Pixel 9 Pro, MacBook Pro, Dell XPS 15, ThinkPad X1 Carbon, iPad Pro, Galaxy Tab S9, and Pixel Tablet. By the end, you'll know exactly which device deserves a spot in your tech arsenal if sound quality is a priority.

Smartphone Audio Showdown: iPhone 16 Pro Max vs Galaxy S25 Ultra vs Pixel 9 Pro

Smartphones are our most personal devices, and the audio they produce—both from their built-in speakers and through headphones—is crucial. The iPhone 16 Pro Max continues Apple's tradition of excellent speaker tuning. The dual speakers (one bottom-firing, one in the earpiece) produce a surprisingly wide soundstage with crisp highs and a punchy midrange. Bass is present but not overwhelming, which suits most genres. When I played Tame Impala's "The Less I Know the Better," the synths felt airy, and the bass line was tight without muddying the vocals. The Galaxy S25 Ultra, on the other hand, uses a slightly larger speaker enclosure and supports Dolby Atmos. The result is a more immersive experience, especially for movies. The sound is louder and more aggressive, with a noticeable boost in low-end frequencies. However, at maximum volume, I noticed slight distortion on complex tracks—something the iPhone handled better. The Pixel 9 Pro, while not as loud as the Galaxy, offers a more balanced and natural sound profile. It's less bass-heavy but excels at vocal clarity, making it a great choice for podcasts and audiobooks.

When it comes to wired audio, none of these phones have a headphone jack, so you'll need a USB-C dongle. The iPhone uses a Lightning-to-3.5mm adapter (or USB-C in the EU version), and its built-in DAC is decent but not audiophile-grade. The Galaxy S25 Ultra supports high-resolution audio via USB-C and includes a more capable DAC, which I noticed when using a pair of wired IEMs—the soundstage felt wider and more detailed. The Pixel 9 Pro falls in between, but its Bluetooth implementation is where it shines. It supports LDAC and aptX Adaptive, which, when paired with a high-quality pair of wireless earbuds, can deliver near-lossless audio. The iPhone only supports AAC, which is fine for Apple Music but not as flexible for Android users. In real-world testing, the Galaxy S25 Ultra gave me the most control over audio quality, but the iPhone was the most consistent and distortion-free at high volumes.

Laptop Audio: MacBook Pro vs Dell XPS 15 vs ThinkPad X1 Carbon

Laptops are often used for media consumption and creative work, so speaker quality matters. The MacBook Pro (14-inch, M3 Pro) has long been the gold standard for laptop audio. Its six-speaker system with force-cancelling woofers produces a rich, full-bodied sound that rivals many external speakers. When I played Hans Zimmer's "Time" from Inception, the orchestral swells were dynamic, and the bass had a physical presence that I rarely feel from a laptop. The Dell XPS 15 (2025 model) also impresses with its quad-speaker setup tuned by Waves. It's slightly less powerful than the MacBook, but the soundstage is wide, and the mids are clear. However, at higher volumes, the XPS tends to get a bit harsh in the upper frequencies. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon, known for business use, has a more modest audio system. Its speakers are adequate for conference calls and casual listening, but they lack the depth and clarity of the other two. The bass is almost nonexistent, and the overall sound is thin. If you're a music producer or a movie buff, the MacBook Pro is the clear winner. For a balance of portability and decent sound, the XPS 15 is a solid choice, but the ThinkPad is best left for productivity tasks where audio is secondary.

Headphone output is another area where these laptops diverge. The MacBook Pro's 3.5mm jack is surprisingly powerful, with a clean signal that can drive high-impedance headphones (up to 300 ohms) without an external amp. The XPS 15 also has a good DAC, but it's not as robust—my 250-ohm Sennheiser HD 600s sounded a bit underpowered. The ThinkPad's audio jack is basic, fine for earbuds but not for serious listening. For Bluetooth, all three support aptX and AAC, but the MacBook's implementation is more stable, with less latency when using wireless earbuds. In my tests, the MacBook Pro delivered the most consistent and enjoyable audio experience, whether through speakers or headphones.

Tablet Audio: iPad Pro vs Galaxy Tab S9 vs Pixel Tablet

Tablets are increasingly used for streaming and gaming, so their speakers need to be impressive. The iPad Pro (12.9-inch) features a four-speaker system that automatically adjusts based on orientation. The sound is incredibly immersive, with a wide soundstage and excellent separation. When I watched the opening scene of "Dune," the low rumble of the sandworm was palpable, and the dialogue remained crisp. The Galaxy Tab S9 also has four speakers tuned by AKG and supports Dolby Atmos. It's slightly louder than the iPad, but the bass is a bit boomy, which can muddy complex tracks. The Pixel Tablet, which doubles as a smart display, has a more modest dual-speaker setup. It's fine for casual listening, but it lacks the depth and clarity of the other two. For music, the iPad Pro is the most accurate, while the Galaxy Tab S9 is better for action movies due to its extra volume. The Pixel Tablet is best for podcasts and background music in the kitchen.

All three tablets support high-resolution Bluetooth codecs, but the iPad Pro only supports AAC, which is a limitation for Android users who prefer LDAC. The Galaxy Tab S9 supports LDAC and aptX, making it more versatile for audiophiles. The Pixel Tablet also supports aptX, but its speaker quality holds it back. In terms of headphone use, none of these tablets have a headphone jack, so you'll need a USB-C dongle. The iPad Pro's USB-C port has a decent DAC, but the Galaxy Tab S9's is slightly better, offering more headroom. For most users, the iPad Pro is the best all-around tablet for audio, but if you're an Android user who values codec flexibility, the Galaxy Tab S9 is a strong contender.

The Specs That Actually Matter for Audio

When comparing audio performance, it's easy to get lost in marketing jargon. But there are a few key specs that genuinely impact sound quality. First, the DAC (digital-to-analog converter) determines how accurately your device converts digital audio files into analog signals. A high-quality DAC, like the ESS Sabre or Cirrus Logic chips found in some laptops and phones, can reduce distortion and improve detail. Second, the amplifier (amp) provides power to your headphones or speakers. A more powerful amp can drive high-impedance headphones without clipping. Third, speaker drivers—their size, material, and number—affect frequency response and soundstage. Larger drivers typically produce better bass, while multiple drivers allow for stereo separation. Fourth, Bluetooth codecs matter: LDAC and aptX Adaptive offer higher bitrates than AAC, preserving more detail in wireless audio. Finally, software tuning (like Dolby Atmos or EQ presets) can enhance the listening experience, but it's subjective. In my testing, devices with dedicated audio hardware (like the MacBook Pro's high-impedance headphone jack) consistently outperformed those that rely on generic components.

Real-World Performance: Music, Movies, and Calls

To give you a clear picture, I conducted a series of tests across all nine devices. For music, I used a playlist of lossless tracks spanning rock, jazz, and electronic genres. The iPhone 16 Pro Max and MacBook Pro delivered the most accurate reproduction, with the MacBook edging out the iPhone in bass depth. The Galaxy S25 Ultra and Galaxy Tab S9 were more exciting but less precise, with a slight V-shaped EQ that flatters pop music. The Pixel 9 Pro and Pixel Tablet were the most neutral, which some listeners may find boring. For movies, I watched the same action sequence on each device. The iPad Pro and MacBook Pro provided the most cinematic experience, with clear dialogue and impactful effects. The Galaxy devices were louder but had a slight echo in the midrange. The ThinkPad and Pixel Tablet were underwhelming, with muffled sound at higher volumes. For phone calls, I tested each device in a noisy café. The iPhone and Pixel 9 Pro had the best noise cancellation, making my voice clear to the caller. The Galaxy S25 Ultra was close, but the MacBook Pro (using its built-in mic) was surprisingly good, though not as portable. The ThinkPad's mic was adequate, but the speakers were too quiet for hands-free use.

Value for Money: Which Devices Give You the Best Audio Bang for Your Buck

Audio quality is often a premium feature, but some devices offer better value than others. Among smartphones, the Pixel 9 Pro is the most affordable of the three, and its balanced sound and excellent call quality make it a great choice for budget-conscious audiophiles. The Galaxy S25 Ultra costs more but adds LDAC support and a more powerful amp, which justifies the price for wireless enthusiasts. The iPhone 16 Pro Max is the most expensive, but its consistent performance and integration with Apple Music make it worth it for iOS users. In laptops, the MacBook Pro is the clear winner for audio, but it's also the priciest. The Dell XPS 15 offers 80% of the MacBook's audio quality at a lower price, making it the best value for Windows users. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon is the cheapest, but its audio is a weak point—if you need a business laptop, you're better off using external speakers. For tablets, the iPad Pro is the most expensive but delivers the best audio. The Galaxy Tab S9 is slightly cheaper and offers more codec options, making it a better value for Android users. The Pixel Tablet is the most affordable, but its audio is only passable—you're paying for the smart display features, not the sound. Ultimately, if audio is a top priority, I recommend the MacBook Pro for laptops, the iPhone 16 Pro Max for phones, and the iPad Pro for tablets. But if you're on a budget, the Pixel 9 Pro and Dell XPS 15 offer surprisingly good sound for the price.

Conclusion

After weeks of testing, it's clear that audio quality is not a one-size-fits-all metric. The best device for you depends on your listening habits, your ecosystem, and your budget. The iPhone 16 Pro Max and MacBook Pro set the standard for accuracy and consistency, while the Galaxy S25 Ultra and Galaxy Tab S9 offer more excitement and flexibility. The Pixel 9 Pro and Pixel Tablet are the dark horses, providing clean, neutral sound at a lower cost. And while the ThinkPad X1 Carbon and Dell XPS 15 have their strengths, they fall short in the audio department compared to their rivals. My advice: don't just look at the spec sheet—listen to the devices yourself. Play your favorite tracks, watch a movie, and make a call. The difference is often subtle, but for those who care about sound, it's the difference between hearing music and feeling it. Whichever device you choose, remember that great audio enhances every moment you spend with your tech. So go ahead, press play, and enjoy the show.
