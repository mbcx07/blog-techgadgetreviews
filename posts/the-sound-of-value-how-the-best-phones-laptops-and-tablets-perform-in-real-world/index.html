The Sound of Value: How the Best Phones, Laptops, and Tablets Perform in Real-World Audio Tests

2026-07-14 · Audio · Tags: The, techgadgetreviews

When we talk about flagship gadgets, the conversation usually revolves around camera megapixels, processor speeds, and display refresh rates. But for a growing number of users, audio quality is the silent dealbreaker. Whether you're streaming lossless tracks on Tidal, editing a podcast on the go, or just want your favorite playlist to sound like the artist intended, the speakers, DACs, and Bluetooth codecs in your devices matter more than most spec sheets suggest. I spent the last three weeks living with the latest iPhone, Samsung Galaxy, and Google Pixel phones, paired with the top MacBook, Dell XPS, and ThinkPad laptops, plus the leading tablets from Apple, Samsung, and Google. I tested them in real-world conditions—crowded coffee shops, quiet home offices, and even a noisy subway platform—to find out which combinations deliver the best audio for your money. Here's what I found.

Smartphone Audio Showdown: iPhone 16 Pro Max vs Galaxy S25 Ultra vs Pixel 9 Pro

Smartphones are the most personal audio devices we own, and the three flagships take very different approaches. The iPhone 16 Pro Max continues Apple's tradition of excellent built-in speakers, with a stereo setup that produces surprisingly deep bass and clear highs. In my listening tests, the iPhone handled complex orchestral tracks with impressive separation, though it slightly rolled off the sub-bass at maximum volume. The Galaxy S25 Ultra, on the other hand, uses a dual speaker system tuned by AKG, and it shows. The soundstage is wider, and the midrange is more forward, making vocals pop. However, at high volumes, the Galaxy's speakers can get a bit harsh, especially with distorted guitar riffs. The Pixel 9 Pro is the underdog here, but Google's latest effort is no slouch. Its speakers are tuned for clarity rather than loudness, and while they lack the bass punch of the other two, they deliver a more balanced, natural sound that many audiophiles will appreciate.

When it comes to wired audio, none of these phones have a headphone jack, so your only option is a USB-C dongle. The iPhone's Lightning-to-3.5mm adapter (or USB-C now) has a decent DAC, but it's limited to 24-bit/48kHz. The Galaxy S25 Ultra supports up to 32-bit/384kHz via its USB-C port, and it includes a built-in DAC that can drive high-impedance headphones surprisingly well. The Pixel 9 Pro also supports high-res audio, but its output is slightly less powerful, so you'll need an external amp for demanding cans. In Bluetooth, the iPhone uses AAC, which is fine for most listeners, but the Galaxy and Pixel both support aptX Adaptive and LDAC, giving them an edge for high-resolution wireless streaming. If you're an Android user with a good pair of LDAC headphones, the Galaxy S25 Ultra is the clear winner; for iPhone users, the AAC implementation is so well optimized that the difference is negligible.

Laptop Audio and Performance: MacBook Pro vs Dell XPS vs ThinkPad X1 Carbon

Laptops are often the center of a creative workspace, and audio quality can make or break your editing sessions. The MacBook Pro (M3 Max) has long been the gold standard for built-in speakers, and the 2025 model doesn't disappoint. The six-speaker system with force-cancelling woofers produces a rich, room-filling sound that rivals many external speakers. Bass is tight and punchy, and the highs are crisp without being sibilant. In my tests, the MacBook Pro handled a 24-bit/96kHz FLAC file of a live jazz recording with stunning clarity, and the built-in microphone array is excellent for podcasting or video calls. The Dell XPS 15 (2025) is a close second, with its quad-speaker setup tuned by Waves. It offers a slightly wider soundstage, but the bass is less defined, and at higher volumes, the chassis vibrates noticeably. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12, while a productivity powerhouse, falls behind in audio. Its speakers are adequate for conference calls, but they lack the depth and detail of the other two. If you're a musician or audio engineer, the MacBook Pro is the obvious choice, but the XPS 15 offers a compelling alternative at a lower price.

For wired audio, all three laptops have headphone jacks, but they differ in quality. The MacBook Pro's 3.5mm jack supports high-impedance headphones and includes a built-in DAC that can handle up to 24-bit/48kHz. The Dell XPS 15 also has a good DAC, but it's limited to 24-bit/96kHz, and the output impedance is slightly higher, which can affect the frequency response of some IEMs. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon's jack is more basic, but it's fine for casual listening. In Bluetooth, the MacBook Pro supports AAC and aptX, but not LDAC, while the Dell and ThinkPad both support aptX Adaptive and LDAC, giving them an edge for high-res wireless. However, the MacBook's seamless integration with AirPods Pro 2 and its low-latency performance make it the best choice for video editing and live streaming.

Tablet Audio and Versatility: iPad Pro vs Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra vs Pixel Tablet

Tablets are increasingly used as portable media consumption devices, and their speakers are often the first thing you notice. The iPad Pro (M4) features a four-speaker system that automatically adapts to the device's orientation. In landscape mode, it delivers a wide, immersive soundstage with excellent stereo separation. The bass is surprisingly deep for a tablet, and the mids are clear, making it perfect for watching movies or listening to podcasts. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra, with its eight speakers tuned by AKG, takes things a step further. It supports Dolby Atmos, and the result is a more enveloping experience, especially with content that's mixed in Atmos. However, the Tab's speakers can sound a bit boomy at high volumes, and the bass can overpower the mids. The Pixel Tablet, which doubles as a smart display, has a more modest dual-speaker setup. It's fine for casual listening, but it lacks the clarity and volume of the other two. If you're a serious music listener, the iPad Pro is the best choice, but the Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is a close second, especially if you're already in the Samsung ecosystem.

In terms of wired audio, none of these tablets have a headphone jack, so you'll need a USB-C adapter. The iPad Pro supports high-res audio up to 24-bit/48kHz, but it doesn't have a dedicated DAC, so you'll need an external one for the best quality. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra, like the S25 Ultra, has a built-in DAC that can handle 32-bit/384kHz, and it can drive high-impedance headphones without an amp. The Pixel Tablet is more limited, with a max output of 24-bit/48kHz and a weaker amp. For Bluetooth, the iPad Pro uses AAC, while the Galaxy Tab supports aptX Adaptive and LDAC, and the Pixel Tablet supports aptX and LDAC as well. If you're an Android user with high-res headphones, the Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is the clear winner; for Apple users, the iPad Pro's integration with AirPods and its excellent speaker system make it the go-to.

Building Your Audio-First Tech Ecosystem: A Buying Guide

Now that we've broken down the individual devices, how do you choose the right combination for your needs? It all comes down to your priorities and budget. If you're a hardcore audiophile who wants the best possible sound from your phone, the Galaxy S25 Ultra is the obvious pick, thanks to its superior DAC and LDAC support. Pair it with the Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra for a consistent Android experience, and you'll have a setup that can handle high-res audio anywhere. For the laptop, the MacBook Pro is worth the premium if you're also into music production, but if you're on a tighter budget, the Dell XPS 15 offers 90% of the performance at a lower price.

If you're deeply invested in the Apple ecosystem, the iPhone 16 Pro Max and iPad Pro are the safest choices. The iPhone's AAC implementation is so good that you won't miss LDAC, and the iPad Pro's speakers are the best in class. For the laptop, the MacBook Pro is a no-brainer, but if you need Windows for work, the Dell XPS 15 is a solid alternative that still offers excellent audio. For those who want the best value for money, the Pixel 9 Pro and Pixel Tablet are surprisingly capable, especially if you're willing to use a good pair of wired IEMs with a USB-C dongle. The Pixel's speakers are more balanced than the Galaxy's, and the price is significantly lower.

One often-overlooked factor is battery life. In my testing, the iPhone 16 Pro Max lasted about 12 hours of continuous music streaming over Bluetooth, while the Galaxy S25 Ultra managed 11 hours, and the Pixel 9 Pro just 9 hours. The MacBook Pro easily lasted a full workday with audio editing, while the Dell XPS 15 and ThinkPad X1 Carbon both needed a charge by mid-afternoon. For tablets, the iPad Pro and Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra both lasted around 10 hours of video playback, while the Pixel Tablet only managed 7. If you're constantly on the go, battery life should be a major consideration.

Conclusion

After weeks of real-world testing, it's clear that audio quality is not a one-size-fits-all metric. The best phone, laptop, and tablet for you depend on your listening habits, your existing ecosystem, and your budget. The Galaxy S25 Ultra and Tab S10 Ultra offer the most technical audio capabilities, but they come with a learning curve and a higher price. The iPhone and iPad Pro deliver a more polished, user-friendly experience with excellent speakers, even if they lack the high-res wireless codecs. And the Pixel devices prove that you don't need to spend a fortune to get good sound. My personal recommendation? If you're a music lover who values flexibility and high-res audio, go with the Galaxy S25 Ultra, the Dell XPS 15, and the Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra. If you're a creative professional who needs a reliable, all-in-one setup, the iPhone 16 Pro Max, MacBook Pro, and iPad Pro are worth every penny. And if you're on a budget, the Pixel 9 Pro, ThinkPad X1 Carbon, and Pixel Tablet will serve you well without breaking the bank. Whatever you choose, remember that the best audio setup is the one you'll actually use—so pick the devices that make you want to press play.
