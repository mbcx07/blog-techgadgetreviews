Beyond the Specs: Real-World Audio Performance of 2025's Top Phones, Laptops, and Tablets

2026-07-28 · Audio · Tags: Beyond, techgadgetreviews, audio

When we talk about flagship smartphones, premium laptops, and high-end tablets, the conversation almost always centers on processors, camera megapixels, and display refresh rates. But there's a silent hero that can make or break your daily experience: audio. Whether you're streaming lossless tracks, hopping on a critical video call, or losing yourself in a cinematic game, the sound quality of your device matters more than the spec sheet suggests. Over the past month, I've spent countless hours with the latest iPhone, Galaxy, Pixel, MacBook, XPS, ThinkPad, and a trio of top-tier tablets—not just running benchmarks, but actually listening. Here's how they stack up in the real world, and what you should buy if your ears are as demanding as your eyes.

Smartphone Audio Face-Off: iPhone 16 Pro Max vs Galaxy S25 Ultra vs Pixel 9 Pro

Smartphones have become our primary music players, podcast machines, and hands-free call devices. Yet each flagship takes a different approach to audio. The iPhone 16 Pro Max continues to rely on its dual-speaker setup with spatial audio support, while the Galaxy S25 Ultra boasts a tuned AKG system with Dolby Atmos. The Pixel 9 Pro, meanwhile, focuses on computational audio and adaptive sound. In my listening tests, I played a mix of bass-heavy hip-hop, acoustic vocals, and orchestral pieces at 50% and 100% volume, measuring both subjective quality and objective loudness.

The iPhone delivers the most balanced sound profile—crisp highs, warm mids, and surprisingly deep bass for its size. Its spatial audio creates a wide soundstage that makes movies feel immersive. The Galaxy S25 Ultra, however, gets louder and adds a bit more punch in the low end, but at max volume, the highs can get slightly harsh. The Pixel 9 Pro is the most neutral of the trio, with excellent clarity for podcasts and voice calls, but it lacks the bass presence of its rivals. For Bluetooth, all three support LDAC and AAC, but the Galaxy also includes aptX Adaptive, which is a win for Android users with high-end earbuds. None of them have a headphone jack, so your wired options are limited to USB-C dongles—the iPhone's Lightning-to-3.5mm adapter is still a separate purchase, which feels dated.

  • Best for music lovers: iPhone 16 Pro Max (balanced, immersive)
  • Best for loud environments: Galaxy S25 Ultra (max volume, bass boost)
  • Best for calls and podcasts: Pixel 9 Pro (clarity, adaptive sound)

If you're a serious audiophile, you'll want to pair any of these with a dedicated DAC and wired IEMs, but for everyday listening, the iPhone edges out the competition with its refined tuning and spatial audio implementation.

Laptop Audio: MacBook Pro 16 vs Dell XPS 16 vs ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12

Laptops are often judged by their keyboards and displays, but the speaker system can transform a workhorse into an entertainment hub. The MacBook Pro 16 has long been the gold standard with its six-speaker array and force-cancelling woofers. The Dell XPS 16 now features a quad-speaker setup with Waves Nx audio, while the ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12 relies on Dolby Atmos and dual speakers. I tested each with a variety of content: a Netflix movie, a Zoom call, and a high-bitrate FLAC file.

The MacBook Pro is in a league of its own. The soundstage is wide, the bass is genuinely present, and even at 80% volume, there's no distortion. It handles complex mixes without muddiness, making it ideal for music production or casual listening. The Dell XPS 16 is a close second—its speakers are loud and clear, but the bass is less defined, and the high frequencies can become piercing at max volume. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon, despite its business pedigree, surprised me with its Dolby Atmos tuning. It's excellent for voice calls and conference meetings, with a dedicated microphone array that cancels background noise effectively. However, for music, it falls short of the other two—the sound is thinner and lacks depth.

All three laptops have headphone jacks, which is a relief. The MacBook Pro's 3.5mm jack supports high-impedance headphones, while the XPS and ThinkPad are more standard. For Bluetooth, the MacBook supports AAC and aptX, but not LDAC; the XPS and ThinkPad both support aptX Adaptive, which is better for Android users. If you're a creative professional who lives in audio, the MacBook Pro is worth every penny. If you're a business user who needs clear calls and occasional media, the ThinkPad offers the best value.

Tablet Audio: iPad Pro 13 vs Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra vs Surface Pro 11

Tablets have become the go-to devices for streaming and gaming, and their speakers are often the unsung heroes. The iPad Pro 13 features a four-speaker system with spatial audio, the Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra has eight speakers tuned by AKG, and the Surface Pro 11 uses dual speakers with Dolby Atmos. I watched the same action movie scene on each, then played a bass-heavy track to test low-end response.

The iPad Pro delivers a remarkably immersive experience. The speakers fire from all four corners, creating a true surround effect that makes you forget you're not wearing headphones. The bass is surprisingly punchy for a tablet, and the mids are clear for dialogue. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra, with its eight speakers, is louder and has a wider soundstage, but the tuning is slightly V-shaped—bass and treble are boosted, which can make vocals sound recessed. The Surface Pro 11 is the weakest of the trio; its speakers are adequate for video calls and light media, but they lack the volume and clarity for serious listening. It also has a headphone jack, which the iPad and Galaxy Tab lack (they rely on USB-C or Bluetooth).

For tablet audio, the iPad Pro is the clear winner if you prioritize a balanced, immersive sound. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is better for those who want maximum volume and don't mind a bit of coloration. The Surface Pro 11 is best for productivity, not entertainment—its audio is functional but forgettable.

The Buying Guide: Matching Audio to Your Lifestyle

Now that you've seen the head-to-head comparisons, how do you choose? It all comes down to how you use your devices. If you're a commuter who listens to music on the go, your smartphone's speakers matter less than its Bluetooth codec support and battery life. The Galaxy S25 Ultra's aptX Adaptive is a boon if you own high-end wireless earbuds, while the iPhone's AAC is universally compatible but not lossless over Bluetooth. For wired listening, you'll need a USB-C DAC—the Apple dongle is decent, but a dedicated portable DAC like the DragonFly Cobalt will elevate any phone.

For laptop buyers, consider your primary use case. If you're a content creator or music producer, the MacBook Pro's audio output is unmatched, and its headphone jack can drive high-impedance cans. If you're a business professional who spends hours on video calls, the ThinkPad X1 Carbon's microphone array and Dolby Atmos tuning will make you sound crystal clear. The Dell XPS 16 is a great middle ground—it's a multimedia powerhouse that handles both work and play, but its speakers are slightly less refined than the MacBook's.

Tablet buyers should think about whether they'll use the tablet as a primary media device or a secondary screen. The iPad Pro is the best for streaming and gaming, with its spatial audio and high-quality speakers. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is a close second, especially if you're already in the Android ecosystem and want a larger display. The Surface Pro 11 is a laptop replacement first, so its audio is an afterthought—if you need a tablet for note-taking and light browsing, it's fine, but don't expect a cinematic experience.

Value for money is also crucial. The iPhone 16 Pro Max and MacBook Pro 16 are premium-priced, but their audio performance justifies the cost for enthusiasts. The Galaxy S25 Ultra and Dell XPS 16 offer similar features at slightly lower prices, making them better value for most people. The Pixel 9 Pro and ThinkPad X1 Carbon are the budget-friendly picks in their categories, but they compromise on audio quality—so only choose them if your audio needs are modest.

Conclusion

After weeks of real-world testing, one thing is clear: audio is not a spec you can ignore. The best devices in 2025 don't just look good on paper—they sound good in practice. The iPhone 16 Pro Max leads the smartphone pack with its balanced, immersive sound, while the MacBook Pro 16 remains the undisputed king of laptop audio. For tablets, the iPad Pro 13 is the go-to for media lovers. But the right choice ultimately depends on your lifestyle and budget. If you're willing to invest in a dedicated DAC and high-quality headphones, even the weaker speakers can be overcome. But for most of us, the built-in audio is what we'll use daily—so choose wisely. Your ears will thank you.
