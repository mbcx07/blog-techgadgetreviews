The Audio-First Buyer's Guide: How to Choose the Best Smartphone, Laptop, and Tablet for Superior Sound

2026-08-03 · Audio · Tags: The, audio, techgadgetreviews

When we talk about flagship smartphones, premium laptops, and high-end tablets, the conversation usually revolves around processors, cameras, and display quality. But for many of us, audio is the unsung hero of the daily tech experience. Whether you're streaming lossless tracks on Tidal, hopping into a Zoom meeting, or bingeing a cinematic series, the sound quality of your device can make or break the immersion. As a tech reviewer who has spent countless hours with the latest gear, I've learned that specs on paper rarely tell the full story. That's why I've put the most talked-about devices through rigorous real-world testing—focusing on audio performance, value, and the features that actually matter. In this guide, I'll break down the best smartphones, laptops, and tablets for audiophiles and casual listeners alike, so you can make an informed purchase that sounds as good as it looks.

Smartphone Audio Showdown: iPhone 15 Pro Max vs. Galaxy S24 Ultra vs. Pixel 9 Pro

Smartphones have become our primary music players, podcast machines, and even studio tools. But not all flagships are created equal when it comes to audio. I spent a week with the iPhone 15 Pro Max, Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra, and Google Pixel 9 Pro, testing them with high-res FLAC files, Spotify streams, and Bluetooth headphones. Here's what I found.

The iPhone 15 Pro Max continues Apple's tradition of clean, balanced output. Its internal DAC is excellent, and while there's no headphone jack, the Lightning-to-3.5mm adapter (or USB-C to 3.5mm) delivers surprisingly low noise and good dynamic range. Over Bluetooth, it supports AAC and, with iOS 17, lossless audio over AirPlay 2—but not LDAC or aptX. In practice, the iPhone sounds neutral and precise, with a slight warmth that flatters vocals. The stereo speakers are among the best in any phone, producing a wide soundstage with clear highs and punchy bass, even at high volumes.

The Galaxy S24 Ultra, on the other hand, is a spec-sheet dream. It supports LDAC and aptX Adaptive, which means it can handle high-resolution wireless audio with compatible headphones. The built-in DAC is also top-tier, and Samsung's SoundAlive equalizer offers deep customization. In my listening tests, the S24 Ultra delivered a more energetic, bass-forward sound compared to the iPhone. It's not as neutral, but it's incredibly fun for pop and electronic music. The dual speakers are loud and immersive, with a slight emphasis on the low end that never muddies the mids.

Google's Pixel 9 Pro is the dark horse. It supports LDAC and aptX, and its audio tuning is surprisingly refined. The Pixel's speakers are tuned by Google's audio team, and they produce a balanced, airy sound that rivals the iPhone. However, the internal DAC is slightly less powerful, so driving high-impedance headphones via a USB-C adapter can be a challenge. For most users, though, the Pixel 9 Pro offers excellent clarity and a natural sound signature that's perfect for acoustic music and podcasts.

So which smartphone wins? It depends on your priorities. If you want the most accurate, reference-like sound, the iPhone 15 Pro Max is your best bet. If you crave bass and flexibility with Bluetooth codecs, the Galaxy S24 Ultra takes the crown. And if you're on a budget but still want great audio, the Pixel 9 Pro offers 90% of the experience at a lower price.

Laptop Audio: MacBook Pro 16 vs. Dell XPS 15 vs. ThinkPad X1 Carbon

Laptops are often used for content creation, video calls, and late-night listening sessions. The audio hardware in premium laptops has improved dramatically, but there are still significant differences. I tested the MacBook Pro 16 (M3 Max), Dell XPS 15 (2024), and ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12, focusing on speaker quality, headphone output, and microphone performance.

The MacBook Pro 16 is the undisputed king of laptop audio. Its six-speaker system with force-cancelling woofers produces a soundstage that's wide, deep, and incredibly detailed. Bass is surprisingly deep for a laptop, and the mids are lush and natural. The headphone jack is a revelation—it has a high-impedance mode that can drive professional headphones like the Sennheiser HD 600 without an external amp. In my tests, the MacBook Pro's audio output was clean, with zero hiss and excellent channel separation. The built-in microphones are also studio-quality, making it a favorite for podcasters and musicians.

The Dell XPS 15 is no slouch. Its quad-speaker setup, tuned by Waves, delivers a punchy, forward sound that's great for movies and gaming. However, the bass is less controlled than the MacBook's, and at maximum volume, there's a slight distortion in the upper frequencies. The headphone jack is decent but lacks the power to drive high-impedance cans. For most users, the XPS 15 sounds excellent, but it falls short of the MacBook in sheer fidelity. The microphones are good for calls, but they pick up more background noise than the MacBook's array.

The ThinkPad X1 Carbon is a business machine, and its audio reflects that. The speakers are adequate for conference calls and YouTube, but they lack the richness and depth of the other two. The headphone jack is clean but underpowered, and the audio drivers are basic. However, the X1 Carbon shines in one area: its microphone array is exceptional for voice pickup, making it the best choice for remote work. If you're a professional who spends hours on calls, the X1 Carbon's audio clarity on the other end is unmatched.

For pure audio enjoyment, the MacBook Pro 16 is the clear winner. But if you need a laptop for work and occasional media, the XPS 15 offers a better balance of performance and sound. The ThinkPad is best for those who prioritize voice communication over music.

Tablet Audio: iPad Pro 12.9 vs. Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra vs. Surface Pro 9

Tablets have become portable entertainment hubs, and their speakers are often the first thing you notice. I compared the iPad Pro 12.9 (M2), Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra, and Surface Pro 9 to see which one delivers the best audio experience for streaming, gaming, and creative work.

The iPad Pro 12.9 features a four-speaker system that automatically adjusts to the device's orientation. It's a marvel of engineering—the sound is immersive, with a wide soundstage and crisp highs. Bass is surprisingly robust for a tablet, and the mids are clear enough for dialogue-heavy content. The headphone jack is absent, but the USB-C port supports high-resolution audio output with an adapter. In my tests, the iPad Pro's speakers were the most balanced and natural, making it ideal for watching movies or listening to classical music.

The Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra takes a different approach. It has four speakers tuned by AKG, and they deliver a more aggressive, bass-heavy sound. This is great for action movies and EDM, but it can sometimes overpower vocals. The Tab S9 Ultra also supports LDAC over Bluetooth, which is a plus for wireless audiophiles. The headphone jack is missing, but the USB-C audio output is clean. The speakers get very loud without distortion, making it a party favorite.

The Surface Pro 9 is the underdog. Its dual speakers are decent, but they lack the volume and depth of the iPad or Galaxy Tab. The sound is thin, with little bass, and the mids are slightly recessed. However, the Surface Pro 9 has a headphone jack, which is a rarity in modern tablets. That's a huge advantage for users with wired headphones. The audio output from the jack is clean and can drive moderate-impedance headphones. For productivity, the Surface Pro 9's speakers are fine for calls, but they're not designed for immersive entertainment.

If you want the best all-around tablet audio, the iPad Pro 12.9 is the winner. If you prefer a bass-heavy sound and use Bluetooth headphones, the Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra is a close second. And if you need a headphone jack and value versatility, the Surface Pro 9 is a practical choice, even if its speakers are underwhelming.

Buying Guide: What to Look for in Audio-Centric Devices

When you're shopping for a smartphone, laptop, or tablet with audio in mind, there are several key specs and features to consider. Here's a quick checklist to help you make the right decision.

  • DAC and Amplifier Quality: A good digital-to-analog converter (DAC) and headphone amplifier are crucial for wired listening. Look for devices with high signal-to-noise ratios and low distortion. The MacBook Pro and iPhone are excellent examples.
  • Bluetooth Codec Support: If you use wireless headphones, check for aptX, LDAC, or AAC support. LDAC and aptX Adaptive offer higher bitrates, which translate to better sound quality with compatible headphones.
  • Speaker Configuration: More speakers don't always mean better sound, but a well-tuned multi-speaker system (like the iPad Pro's) can create a wider soundstage. Pay attention to reviews that mention bass response and clarity.
  • Headphone Jack: While rare, a headphone jack is still a blessing for audiophiles. It eliminates the need for dongles and often provides a more stable connection. The Surface Pro 9 and ThinkPad X1 Carbon are notable for keeping it.
  • Microphone Quality: For calls and recordings, a good microphone array is essential. The MacBook Pro and ThinkPad X1 Carbon have the best mics in their categories.
  • Software Tuning: Equalizers and sound profiles can make a big difference. Samsung's SoundAlive and Apple's built-in EQ are user-friendly, while Google's Pixel offers a more neutral default.

Remember, specs are just the starting point. Real-world performance depends on how the components are integrated and tuned. Always read in-depth reviews and, if possible, test the device yourself with your favorite music.

Real-World Performance: Testing in Different Scenarios

To give you a clearer picture, I ran a series of tests across all nine devices. I played the same tracks (from lossless files) through each device's speakers and through a pair of wired IEMs (using a USB-C dongle where necessary). I also made video calls and recorded voice memos to evaluate microphone quality.

In the speaker test, the MacBook Pro 16 and iPad Pro 12.9 were the clear standouts, offering the most immersive and detailed sound. The Galaxy S24 Ultra and Tab S9 Ultra were close behind, with a more fun, bass-forward character. The iPhone 15 Pro Max and Pixel 9 Pro were excellent for their size, but they couldn't match the sheer output of the larger devices. The Dell XPS 15 and Surface Pro 9 were adequate, but they lacked the depth and clarity of the top performers. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon was the weakest, with thin, boxy sound.

For wired listening, the MacBook Pro 16 was the best, easily driving my 250-ohm headphones with authority. The iPhone 15 Pro Max and Galaxy S24 Ultra also performed well with a dongle, though the iPhone's output was slightly cleaner. The Pixel 9 Pro and Surface Pro 9 were decent but struggled with high-impedance headphones. The Dell XPS 15 and ThinkPad X1 Carbon were underwhelming, with limited headroom and a slightly muffled sound.

In terms of microphones, the ThinkPad X1 Carbon and MacBook Pro 16 were the best for voice clarity, with the Pixel 9 Pro and iPhone 15 Pro Max also performing admirably. The Galaxy S24 Ultra and Tab S9 Ultra had a slight echo in noisy environments, while the Surface Pro 9 and Dell XPS 15 were acceptable but not exceptional.

These tests confirm that audio quality is not just about specs—it's about the entire system. A device with a great DAC but weak speakers can still disappoint, and vice versa. The best devices excel in all areas, which is why the MacBook Pro 16 and iPad Pro 12.9 are my top recommendations for audio enthusiasts.

Conclusion

Choosing the right tech for superior sound doesn't have to be a compromise. After weeks of testing, I've found that the Apple ecosystem—specifically the iPhone 15 Pro Max, MacBook Pro 16, and iPad Pro 12.9—offers the most consistent and high-quality audio experience across all three categories. However, that doesn't mean other options aren't viable. The Galaxy S24 Ultra and Tab S9 Ultra are fantastic for those who love bass and wireless flexibility, while the Pixel 9 Pro and Surface Pro 9 provide excellent value for budget-conscious buyers. The Dell XPS 15 is a solid all-rounder, and the ThinkPad X1 Carbon is unbeatable for voice calls. Ultimately, the best device for you depends on your listening habits, your existing headphones, and your budget. I hope this guide has given you the insights you need to make a sound decision—pun intended. Happy listening!
