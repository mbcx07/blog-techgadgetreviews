The Ultimate Audio Showdown: Testing the Best Phones, Laptops, and Tablets for Sound Quality

2026-06-13 · Audio · Tags: The, audio, techgadgetreviews

We live in a world where audio is everywhere—from the podcasts we stream during our commutes to the immersive soundtracks that make our favorite games feel alive. But here's the thing: not all audio is created equal, and the devices we use to consume it play a massive role in how we experience sound. As a tech reviewer, I've spent countless hours with the latest smartphones, laptops, and tablets, and I've noticed a fascinating trend: audio quality has become a battleground for manufacturers. It's no longer just about having a speaker that gets loud; it's about clarity, depth, spatial awareness, and even AI-driven tuning. In this comprehensive guide, I'm going to break down the real-world audio performance of the biggest players in the game—Apple, Samsung, Google, Dell, Lenovo, and more—to help you figure out which device deserves a spot in your pocket or on your desk. We're going beyond the spec sheet and diving into what actually matters when you hit play.

The Smartphone Symphony: iPhone 16 Pro vs. Galaxy S25 Ultra vs. Pixel 9 Pro

When it comes to smartphones, audio is a dual-edged sword. You have the external speakers for hands-free listening and the headphone jack (or lack thereof) for private sessions. Let's start with the external speakers, because that's where the biggest differences lie. The iPhone 16 Pro continues Apple's tradition of excellent built-in speakers. They're punchy, with a surprising amount of bass for such a small chassis, and the stereo separation is wide enough to create a convincing soundstage when you're watching a movie. The mids are clear, and even at max volume, there's minimal distortion. It's a very refined, balanced sound profile that suits almost any genre.

On the other side, the Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra has taken a different approach. Samsung has focused on sheer power and a slightly more V-shaped sound curve, meaning boosted bass and treble. This makes for an incredibly exciting listening experience, especially for pop music or action-packed video games. The bass hits hard, and the highs sparkle, but sometimes the mids can feel a little recessed, which can make vocals sound a bit distant in complex mixes. Meanwhile, the Google Pixel 9 Pro is the dark horse. Google's audio tuning is incredibly neutral, almost studio-monitor-like. It doesn't try to flatter your music; it just presents it accurately. This is fantastic for podcasts and audiobooks, where vocal clarity is paramount, but it might feel a little flat if you're used to the bass-heavy tuning of other flagships.

When it comes to wired and wireless audio, all three have dropped the headphone jack, so you're relying on Bluetooth codecs. Apple uses AAC, Samsung supports a wide range including LDAC and aptX, and Google also supports LDAC. In practice, the Samsung and Pixel will give you higher-resolution wireless audio if you have compatible earbuds, but the iPhone's AAC implementation is so well-optimized that most listeners won't notice a difference in day-to-day use. The real takeaway? For the best all-rounder external speaker, the iPhone 16 Pro wins. For bass lovers, the Galaxy S25 Ultra is your pick. And for purists who want accuracy, the Pixel 9 Pro is a revelation.

Laptop Audio Labs: MacBook Pro vs. Dell XPS 16 vs. ThinkPad X1 Carbon

Laptops are a different beast. You're not just listening to music; you're often in video calls, editing videos, or watching movies on the couch. The MacBook Pro (especially the 14 and 16-inch models) has long been the gold standard for laptop audio. Apple's custom silicon and advanced speaker arrays deliver a sound that is genuinely astonishing. The bass response is deep and authoritative, something you'd expect from a dedicated external speaker, not a thin laptop lid. The soundstage is wide, and the imaging is precise—you can pinpoint where instruments are in the mix. For creative professionals who need to trust their ears, the MacBook Pro is the undisputed king.

Dell's XPS 16 has made significant strides in recent years. The latest models feature a redesigned speaker system with quad speakers and Waves MaxxAudio tuning. The result is a much more dynamic sound than previous generations. It gets incredibly loud without distorting, and the clarity is impressive. However, it still trails the MacBook Pro in terms of sheer bass depth and overall refinement. The highs can occasionally sound a bit harsh at maximum volume. It's a great laptop for entertainment, but it's not quite a professional audio tool.

Then we have the Lenovo ThinkPad X1 Carbon, a business workhorse. Historically, ThinkPads have had mediocre audio, but the latest X1 Carbon Gen 12 has improved things. The speakers are now upward-firing, which helps with clarity, and the Dolby Atmos tuning adds a sense of spaciousness. That said, the audio is still tuned for voice and conference calls, not for music production. The bass is almost non-existent, and the sound profile is very mid-focused. It's perfect for hearing your colleagues clearly on Zoom, but if you want to enjoy a movie on a long flight, you'll definitely want to pack a pair of headphones. For the best all-around laptop audio experience, the MacBook Pro is the clear winner, with the XPS 16 as a strong runner-up for Windows users.

Tablet Tuning: iPad Pro M4 vs. Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra

Tablets have become our go-to devices for media consumption, and audio is a critical part of that experience. The iPad Pro M4 features a four-speaker audio system that is, frankly, overkill in the best way. It adapts to the tablet's orientation, so the speakers always fire correctly whether you're in portrait or landscape. The sound is incredibly balanced, with tight, punchy bass and crystal-clear highs. It's like having a portable soundbar. The volume is impressive, and it handles dynamic range with ease, making it perfect for watching HDR movies on a plane or in bed.

Samsung's Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is a massive device, and it uses that size to its advantage. It has a quad-speaker system tuned by AKG and supports Dolby Atmos. The sound is very loud and immersive, with a slightly more aggressive bass profile than the iPad. This makes it fantastic for action movies and gaming. However, the sound can sometimes feel a bit 'boomy' compared to the iPad's more controlled output. In a head-to-head listening test, the iPad Pro M4 sounds more natural and detailed, while the Tab S10 Ultra sounds more exciting and visceral. It's a classic case of accuracy versus fun. If you're an audiophile who wants to hear the music as the artist intended, the iPad is the better choice. If you want a cinematic, room-filling sound for movies, the Galaxy Tab will not disappoint.

Real-World Audio Tests: What Actually Matters?

Spec sheets tell you about drivers and codecs, but they don't tell you how a device performs in a noisy coffee shop or a quiet bedroom. In my real-world testing, I look for a few key things. First is clarity at low volumes. Many devices sound great at 50% volume but become muddy or harsh at 10%. The iPhone and iPad excel here, maintaining detail even at whisper-quiet levels. Second is distortion at high volumes. The Galaxy S25 Ultra and Tab S10 Ultra can get incredibly loud, but they start to lose their composure at max volume, while the MacBook Pro and iPad Pro remain remarkably clean.

Another critical factor is microphone quality. In our world of endless video calls, the mic matters as much as the speaker. The Pixel 9 Pro has the best noise cancellation of any phone I've tested, making your voice sound clear even in a hurricane. The MacBook Pro also has an excellent studio-quality mic array that makes you sound like you're in a professional podcast studio. On the Windows side, the Dell XPS 16 is decent, but the ThinkPad X1 Carbon still has the best voice pickup for business calls, which is its primary audience. These are the nuances that don't show up in a spec sheet but make a huge difference in your daily life.

Choosing Your Audio Champion: A Buyer's Guide

So, after all this testing, which device should you buy? It really depends on your priorities. If you're a music lover who wants the best external speakers on a smartphone, the iPhone 16 Pro is the safest bet. It's reliable, balanced, and sounds great with everything. If you're a bass head who loves a punchy sound signature, the Galaxy S25 Ultra is your phone. For laptop users, the MacBook Pro is the ultimate choice for anyone who creates or consumes media seriously. The Dell XPS 16 is the best Windows alternative, offering a fantastic screen and great sound for entertainment. And if your laptop is primarily for work and video calls, the ThinkPad X1 Carbon's superior microphone and clear, voice-focused speakers make it the practical choice.

For tablets, the iPad Pro M4 is the gold standard. It's the best media consumption device on the market, period. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is a worthy challenger, especially if you're already in the Samsung ecosystem and prefer a more cinematic, bass-heavy sound. Don't forget to consider your accessories, too. A great pair of wireless earbuds or headphones can elevate any of these devices, but that's a topic for another deep dive. Ultimately, the best device is the one that fits your personal listening habits and your ecosystem. I hope this breakdown helps you make a more informed decision. Happy listening!

Conclusion

Audio is a deeply personal experience, and the devices we choose to play our music, movies, and podcasts are our gateways to that experience. From the refined, balanced sound of the iPhone and iPad Pro to the powerful, bass-forward tuning of the Galaxy S25 Ultra and Tab S10 Ultra, there's a perfect device for every listener. The MacBook Pro continues to dominate the laptop space with its incredible speaker array, while the Dell XPS 16 and ThinkPad X1 Carbon offer compelling alternatives for Windows users with different priorities. I hope this guide has given you a clearer picture of the real-world performance of these gadgets. Remember, the best tech isn't just about the biggest numbers on a spec sheet; it's about the feeling you get when you press play and the sound fills the room. So, choose wisely, and let the music play.
