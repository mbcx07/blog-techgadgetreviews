The Sound of the Future: How Flagship Phones, Laptops, and Tablets Are Redefining Audio in 2025

2026-06-08 · Audio · Tags: techgadgetreviews, The

For years, audio was the forgotten child of the tech world—a spec sheet afterthought buried beneath megapixel counts and processor clock speeds. But that era is officially over. In 2025, the most compelling reason to upgrade your daily driver might not be the camera or the battery life, but the sound system built into your pocket, your lap, and your backpack. We've spent the last month living with the latest flagship smartphones, ultraportable laptops, and premium tablets, pushing their speakers, headphone jacks (or lack thereof), and Bluetooth codecs to the absolute limit. The results are surprising, and they're reshaping how we consume media, game, and work on the go. This isn't just about volume; it's about spatial audio, dynamic range, and the subtle details that make your favorite playlist feel alive. Let's dive into the real-world audio performance of the devices that matter most.

The Smartphone Audio Arms Race: iPhone 16 Pro vs. Galaxy S25 Ultra vs. Pixel 9 Pro

Smartphones are our primary music machines, yet their tiny chassis present an acoustic nightmare. The three titans—Apple, Samsung, and Google—have tackled this problem with radically different philosophies. The iPhone 16 Pro continues to impress with its balanced, studio-tuned drivers. Apple's focus on 'Adaptive Audio' means the speakers automatically adjust to your environment, whether you're in a quiet library or a noisy subway. The mids are incredibly clear, making podcasts and dialogue-heavy content a joy, while the bass, though not thunderous, is surprisingly taut for a device this thin.

In stark contrast, the Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra goes for raw power. Its stereo speakers are noticeably louder, with a wider soundstage that fills a room. If you're watching a blockbuster movie without headphones, the Galaxy is the clear winner—explosions have real weight, and the high-frequency details don't get lost in the mix. However, this power comes at a slight cost to subtlety; classical music can sometimes sound a bit aggressive.

Then there's the Google Pixel 9 Pro. Google has taken a 'software-first' approach, using AI to upscale compressed audio streams in real-time. The result is a cleaner, more detailed sound than the hardware alone should produce. While the physical speaker volume is a notch below the Galaxy, the clarity at moderate volumes is exceptional. For audiophiles who primarily listen to lossless files, the Pixel's processing is a game-changer, revealing layers in recordings you didn't know existed. Ultimately, the 'best' phone speaker depends on your priority: power (Samsung), balance (Apple), or precision (Google).

Laptop Audio: MacBook Pro vs. Dell XPS 16 vs. ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12

Laptops are no longer just for spreadsheets; they're portable theaters. The MacBook Pro (M4) remains the gold standard for built-in speakers, and it's not even close. Apple's six-speaker array with force-cancelling woofers produces a soundstage that defies physics. The bass is deep enough to feel in your chest, and the highs are silky without ever becoming harsh. It's the only laptop we've tested where you might actually forget you're not listening to a dedicated Bluetooth speaker. For video editors and music producers, the MacBook Pro is the obvious choice—what you hear is what you get, with minimal coloration.

Dell's XPS 16 has made significant strides, closing the gap with its own quad-speaker setup tuned by Waves. The sound is punchy and detailed, with a particular strength in the mid-range frequencies that carry vocals. It's excellent for conference calls and rock music, but it lacks the MacBook's sheer depth and room-filling presence. The XPS is a fantastic all-rounder, but it doesn't have that 'wow' factor.

The ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12 is a different beast. It's designed for business, and its audio reflects that. The speakers are forward-facing and incredibly clear for voice reproduction, making them the best for Zoom calls and dictation. However, for music and movies, they fall flat. The bass is almost non-existent, and the soundstage is narrow. If you're a road warrior who lives in meetings, the ThinkPad is perfect. If you want a laptop that doubles as a boombox, look elsewhere.

Tablet Audio Showdown: iPad Pro (M4) vs. Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra vs. Surface Pro 11

Tablets have become our personal cinemas, and the audio hardware has evolved to match. The iPad Pro (M4) features a four-speaker audio system that adapts to how you hold the device. Whether in landscape or portrait, the stereo image remains locked and correct. The sound is incredibly refined, with a wide soundstage and excellent separation between instruments. It's the best tablet for consuming high-fidelity content, period. Apple's Spatial Audio integration with AirPods Pro takes it a step further, creating a truly immersive bubble of sound that tracks your head movement.

Samsung's Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is a multimedia monster. Its massive 14.6-inch screen is matched by a quad-speaker setup tuned by AKG. The volume is staggering, and it supports Dolby Atmos for a virtualized surround-sound experience. For watching Netflix or playing games, the Tab S10 Ultra is arguably more cinematic than the iPad. However, at high volumes, the speakers can distort slightly on complex tracks, and the bass, while powerful, can sometimes overpower the mids.

Microsoft's Surface Pro 11 is the underdog here. It has dual speakers that are better than most laptops but lag behind the dedicated tablets. The sound is clear and serviceable, but it lacks the spatial processing and dynamic range of its rivals. It's fine for background music and YouTube, but if you're buying a tablet primarily for media consumption, the Surface Pro is a compromise. It's a great laptop first, and a decent tablet second.

The Wireless Codec War: LDAC, aptX Lossless, and the Future of Bluetooth

All the speaker hardware in the world is useless if the data reaching it is compressed to death. This is where Bluetooth codecs come in. For years, we were stuck with SBC and AAC, which are fine for casual listening but lose detail. In 2025, the battlefield has shifted. Samsung's Galaxy S25 Ultra supports LDAC and the newer aptX Adaptive, which can stream at up to 24-bit/96kHz. When paired with a high-end pair of earbuds like the Sony WF-1000XM6, the difference is night and day—you hear the air around instruments and the subtle reverb of a recording studio.

Apple remains a walled garden, sticking with AAC. While AAC is highly efficient and sounds great on Apple hardware, it's technically lossy. However, Apple has cleverly sidestepped this with the H2 chip and dynamic head tracking, which makes the audio experience feel more '3D' even if the file isn't lossless. Google is pushing its own LC3 codec for low-power devices, which is promising for battery life but not yet a premium audio standard.

The real winner here is the consumer. We now have multiple high-resolution wireless options that were only available via wired connections a few years ago. When buying your next phone or laptop, check the codec support. If you own high-end wireless headphones, a device that supports LDAC or aptX Lossless will unlock their full potential. If you're all-in on the Apple ecosystem, the seamless integration of AAC and Spatial Audio is a compelling reason to stay.

Value for Money: Which Device Offers the Best Audio Bang for Your Buck?

After weeks of testing, the question isn't 'which is the best,' but 'which is the best for you?' If you're on a budget, the Google Pixel 9 Pro offers 90% of the audio quality of the Galaxy S25 Ultra at a lower price, provided you don't need ear-splitting volume. For laptops, the Dell XPS 16 is the sweet spot—it's cheaper than the MacBook Pro but delivers 85% of its audio performance, making it a better value for most users.

In the tablet world, the iPad Pro (M4) is a luxury item. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra, while expensive, offers a larger screen and more powerful speakers for a similar price, making it a better value for movie buffs. However, if you're a creative professional who relies on accurate audio monitoring, the iPad Pro's neutral sound signature is worth the premium. The key is to match the device to your primary use case:

  • For the commuter: The iPhone 16 Pro or Pixel 9 Pro with a great pair of ANC earbuds.
  • For the media junkie: The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra for cinematic, room-filling sound.
  • For the music producer: The MacBook Pro (M4) for its uncolored, accurate reproduction.
  • For the business traveler: The ThinkPad X1 Carbon for crystal-clear voice calls.

Conclusion: Your Ears Are the Ultimate Judge

The audio landscape in 2025 is more exciting than ever. The gap between dedicated speakers and built-in device audio has narrowed to the point where most people will be perfectly happy ditching their Bluetooth speakers for the sound coming out of their phone or laptop. The iPhone 16 Pro offers a refined, balanced signature; the Galaxy S25 Ultra brings the party; and the MacBook Pro remains the undisputed king of laptop audio. But specs and marketing hype only tell half the story. The best way to choose is to visit a store, play your favorite track on each device, and trust your ears. You'll be surprised at how much the sound of your technology shapes your daily experience. Don't settle for tinny, hollow audio—the future of sound is here, and it's built into your next gadget.
