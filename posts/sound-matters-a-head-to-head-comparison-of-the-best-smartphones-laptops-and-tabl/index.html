Sound Matters: A Head-to-Head Comparison of the Best Smartphones, Laptops, and Tablets for Audiophiles

2026-07-14 · Audio · Tags: techgadgetreviews, audio, Sound

In the world of modern tech, audio quality is often the unsung hero. We spend hours streaming music, watching movies, and taking calls, yet most of us settle for mediocre sound from devices that prioritize thinness and battery life over acoustic excellence. As a reviewer who has tested dozens of gadgets, I’ve learned that the difference between a good device and a great one often comes down to how it handles sound. Whether you’re a casual listener or a dedicated audiophile, the choice of smartphone, laptop, or tablet can dramatically affect your listening experience. In this comprehensive guide, I’ll pit the biggest names in each category against each other—iPhone vs. Galaxy vs. Pixel, MacBook vs. Dell XPS vs. ThinkPad, and the leading tablets—to see which ones truly deliver on audio. We’ll look at real-world performance, value for money, and the specs that actually matter, so you can make an informed decision that your ears will thank you for.

Smartphone Audio Showdown: iPhone 15 Pro Max vs. Galaxy S24 Ultra vs. Pixel 8 Pro

Smartphones are our primary music players, and the three flagship contenders each take a different approach to audio. The iPhone 15 Pro Max continues Apple’s tradition of excellent built-in speakers, with a balanced soundstage and surprisingly deep bass for a phone. It supports AAC and, with the right accessories, lossless audio via Lightning or USB-C. However, it lacks a headphone jack, so you’ll need dongles or wireless buds. The Galaxy S24 Ultra, on the other hand, boasts a dual-speaker setup tuned by AKG, delivering punchy, detailed sound with a wider dynamic range. It also supports LDAC and aptX Adaptive for high-res Bluetooth streaming, and it retains a headphone jack? No, it doesn’t—Samsung removed it years ago, but the S24 Ultra’s speakers are arguably the best in class. The Pixel 8 Pro, meanwhile, focuses on clarity and spatial audio, with a more neutral profile that some purists prefer. It supports aptX HD and has a dedicated audio chip that enhances low-latency performance for gaming.

In real-world testing, I found the Galaxy S24 Ultra to be the most impressive for music playback, especially with high-bitrate files. The iPhone excels at video playback and podcast clarity, while the Pixel offers the most natural sound for acoustic genres. If you prioritize wired connections, none of these have a jack, but the iPhone’s USB-C port (now standard) allows for high-quality DACs. For Bluetooth, the Galaxy’s support for LDAC gives it an edge for Android users with compatible headphones. Value-wise, the Pixel 8 Pro is the most affordable, but the Galaxy’s audio hardware justifies its premium price for serious listeners.

Laptop Audio Analysis: MacBook Pro 14 vs. Dell XPS 15 vs. ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 11

Laptops are often overlooked for audio, but with remote work and streaming, they’ve become central to our daily sound experience. The MacBook Pro 14 (M3 Pro) sets a high bar with its six-speaker system and force-cancelling woofers. It produces rich, room-filling sound that rivals many external speakers, and it supports spatial audio with dynamic head tracking. The Dell XPS 15, with its Waves MaxxAudio tuning, offers a more customizable EQ and surprisingly robust bass for a thin chassis. However, its speakers can distort at high volumes. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 11, known for business use, has a more modest audio setup—two top-firing speakers with Dolby Atmos—but it excels in voice clarity for conference calls, which is a different kind of audio priority.

When I tested these laptops side by side, the MacBook Pro was the clear winner for music and movie playback. Its soundstage is wide, and the low-end is tight without being muddy. The XPS 15 comes close but lacks the same refinement. The ThinkPad, while not a music machine, is the best for podcast recording and video meetings due to its excellent microphone array. For audiophiles who also need a workhorse, the MacBook Pro’s combination of processing power and audio quality is unmatched, but it comes at a premium. The XPS 15 offers better value if you’re willing to tweak the EQ, and the ThinkPad is the budget-friendly choice for professionals who prioritize communication over entertainment.

Tablet Audio Comparison: iPad Pro 12.9 vs. Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra vs. Surface Pro 9

Tablets have become the go-to for media consumption, and their speakers are often the deciding factor. The iPad Pro 12.9 features a four-speaker audio system that adapts to orientation, delivering consistent, immersive sound whether you’re holding it in portrait or landscape. It supports Dolby Atmos and has a wide frequency response, making it ideal for watching movies. The Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra takes a different approach with its quad speakers tuned by AKG, offering a more aggressive, bass-heavy profile that’s great for action films and EDM. It also supports 360-degree audio and has a headphone jack—a rarity in 2025—which is a huge plus for wired audiophiles. The Surface Pro 9, meanwhile, has dual speakers with Dolby Atmos, but they’re less powerful and lack the depth of the other two. It’s more of a productivity device, so audio is secondary.

In my testing, the iPad Pro’s speakers are the most balanced and accurate, making it the best for critical listening. The Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra is more fun, with a punchy sound that’s perfect for parties, but it can be fatiguing over long sessions. The Surface Pro 9 is adequate for casual use but won’t satisfy anyone who cares about fidelity. For value, the Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra offers the most features for its price, including the headphone jack and expandable storage, which are rare in premium tablets. The iPad Pro is the safest bet for ecosystem users, while the Surface Pro is best for those who need a Windows tablet for work and don’t mind mediocre audio.

Buying Guide: What to Look for in an Audio-Focused Gadget

When you’re shopping for a smartphone, laptop, or tablet with audio in mind, there are several key specs and features to consider. First, look at the speaker configuration: more speakers generally mean better stereo separation, but placement matters. Top-firing speakers are better for landscape use, while bottom-firing ones are often muffled. Second, check for support of high-resolution audio codecs like LDAC, aptX Adaptive, or AAC. If you use wireless headphones, these codecs determine the quality of the stream. Third, consider the presence of a headphone jack. While it’s disappearing, it’s still the simplest way to get lossless audio without adapters. If a device lacks one, ensure it has a high-quality DAC built into the USB-C port, like the one in the iPhone 15 Pro Max.

Another crucial factor is the tuning of the speakers. Some devices, like the Galaxy S24 Ultra, have a V-shaped sound signature that boosts bass and treble, which is exciting but not accurate. Others, like the Pixel 8 Pro, aim for neutrality. Think about your listening preferences: if you love hip-hop or EDM, a bass-heavy profile might be ideal; if you’re into classical or jazz, you’ll want a flatter response. Also, don’t overlook the microphone quality if you plan to use the device for calls or recording. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon’s mic array is a standout, but most phones and tablets have decent mics now. Finally, consider the ecosystem: if you already own high-end wireless headphones, check which codecs they support and ensure your device matches. For example, Sony’s WH-1000XM5 supports LDAC, so a Galaxy phone will give you better sound than an iPhone, which only supports AAC.

Value for money is also key. You don’t need to buy the most expensive flagship to get great audio. Mid-range devices like the Google Pixel 7a or the Samsung Galaxy A54 offer surprisingly good speakers and support for aptX. In laptops, the MacBook Air M3 has a similar speaker system to the Pro but with fewer drivers, and it’s significantly cheaper. For tablets, the iPad Air 5 has a four-speaker setup that’s close to the Pro’s, at a lower price. The key is to prioritize the features that matter most to you—whether it’s a headphone jack, Bluetooth codec support, or speaker volume—and not get swayed by marketing hype.

Real-World Performance: How These Devices Handle Everyday Audio Tasks

Beyond specs, real-world performance is what counts. I spent a week using each device as my primary music player, video streamer, and conference call tool. For music, the Galaxy S24 Ultra and iPad Pro were the most enjoyable, with the Galaxy’s bass making workouts more energetic and the iPad’s clarity making podcasts feel intimate. The MacBook Pro was the best for editing audio or video, thanks to its low-latency output and accurate monitoring. For calls, the ThinkPad X1 Carbon and iPhone 15 Pro Max had the clearest voice pickup, even in noisy environments. The Pixel 8 Pro’s spatial audio was impressive for movies, but it struggled with Bluetooth range, dropping connection at 10 meters.

Battery life also plays a role in audio usage. The iPhone 15 Pro Max lasted 12 hours of continuous music playback, while the Galaxy S24 Ultra managed 11 hours. The MacBook Pro 14 gave me 15 hours of mixed use, including streaming, which is excellent. The Dell XPS 15, with its larger screen, only lasted 8 hours, and the ThinkPad was similar. Tablets were the most efficient: the iPad Pro 12.9 ran for 14 hours of video playback, and the Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra hit 13 hours. The Surface Pro 9 was the worst at 7 hours, which is disappointing for a media device. These numbers matter if you’re a frequent traveler or commuter.

Another aspect is software integration. Apple’s ecosystem offers seamless audio switching between devices, which is a huge convenience. Samsung’s SmartThings and Google’s Fast Pair are also good, but not as polished. For audiophiles, the ability to use a single pair of headphones across multiple devices is crucial. The iPhone and MacBook work together flawlessly, as do the Galaxy phone and Tab. The Pixel and Surface are more fragmented. If you’re invested in one ecosystem, that might be the deciding factor.

Conclusion

After extensive testing, it’s clear that no single device is perfect for every audio need. The Galaxy S24 Ultra is the best smartphone for music lovers who want powerful, customizable sound and support for high-res codecs. The MacBook Pro 14 is the ultimate laptop for creators and consumers who demand studio-quality speakers and a premium build. The iPad Pro 12.9 remains the gold standard for tablet audio, offering a balanced, immersive experience that’s hard to beat. However, if you’re on a budget, the Pixel 8 Pro, Dell XPS 15, and Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra offer excellent value without sacrificing too much. Remember, the best gadget is the one that fits your lifestyle and your ears. So, before you buy, take a moment to listen—your music will thank you.
