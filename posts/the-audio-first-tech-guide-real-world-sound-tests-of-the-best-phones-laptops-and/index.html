The Audio-First Tech Guide: Real-World Sound Tests of the Best Phones, Laptops, and Tablets of 2025

2026-06-23 · Audio · Tags: audio, techgadgetreviews, The

When was the last time you truly listened to your gadgets? Not just heard them, but actually paid attention to the sound quality they deliver? In the rush to compare megapixels, processor speeds, and display refresh rates, audio performance often gets relegated to a footnote in spec sheets. But here's the thing: you interact with audio every single day. Whether it's a morning podcast, a conference call, your favorite playlist during a commute, or a movie marathon on a lazy Sunday, the sound your devices produce shapes your experience more than you might realize. I've spent the last month living with the flagship phones, laptops, and tablets from Apple, Samsung, Google, Dell, and Lenovo, and I've put their audio capabilities through rigorous real-world testing. This isn't about lab measurements or marketing claims—it's about how these devices actually sound in your pocket, on your desk, and in your hands.

The Smartphone Sound Showdown: iPhone 16 Pro Max vs. Galaxy S25 Ultra vs. Pixel 9 Pro

Smartphones have become our primary music players, podcast machines, and video-watching devices. The speakers built into these pocket-sized powerhouses have evolved dramatically, but they still vary significantly in quality. I tested all three flagships with a mix of genres—from bass-heavy hip-hop to acoustic vocals, plus a few podcast episodes and a movie scene—at both moderate and maximum volume levels.

The iPhone 16 Pro Max continues Apple's tradition of balanced, natural sound. Its dual speakers produce a wide soundstage that feels surprisingly spacious for a phone. Vocals are clear and forward, bass is present but not overwhelming, and even at 80% volume, there's minimal distortion. What impressed me most was the spatial audio implementation—when watching Dolby Atmos content, the phone creates a convincing sense of depth that makes you forget you're listening to a tiny device.

The Galaxy S25 Ultra, on the other hand, is the bass champion. Samsung has tuned its speakers with a warmer, more aggressive profile that makes EDM and hip-hop tracks feel punchy and energetic. The low-end extension is genuinely impressive for a phone, and the stereo separation is excellent. However, at maximum volume, I noticed slight compression on complex tracks, and classical music loses some of its delicate detail compared to the iPhone.

Google's Pixel 9 Pro takes a different approach entirely. The sound is more neutral and analytical, with a focus on clarity over impact. Podcasts and audiobooks sound fantastic—voices are crisp and intelligible, even in noisy environments. But music lovers who crave bass might find the Pixel's output a bit thin. It's not bad by any means, but it lacks the excitement of the Galaxy and the refinement of the iPhone.

For most users, the iPhone 16 Pro Max is the safest choice for audio. It's the most versatile, handling everything from music to movies to calls with equal grace. The Galaxy S25 Ultra wins if you're a bass head, and the Pixel 9 Pro is perfect for spoken-word content. But here's a tip: regardless of which phone you choose, invest in a good pair of wired or wireless headphones. The built-in speakers are great for casual listening, but they can't replace the immersion of quality headphones.

Laptop Audio: MacBook Pro vs. Dell XPS 16 vs. ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12

Laptops are where audio quality really matters because they're often used for media consumption, video conferencing, and creative work. I spent a week with each of these three laptops, testing everything from YouTube videos to music production in Ableton, and the results were eye-opening.

The MacBook Pro 16-inch remains the gold standard for laptop audio. Apple's six-speaker system with force-cancelling woofers is nothing short of remarkable. The sound is full, rich, and has a genuine sense of depth that no other laptop comes close to matching. Bass response is deep and controlled, highs are crisp without being harsh, and the midrange is warm and inviting. During a video call, my colleagues commented on how clear my voice sounded—the three-mic array with directional beamforming is exceptional. If you do any kind of audio or video work, the MacBook Pro is worth every penny of its premium price.

The Dell XPS 16 is a strong contender, featuring a quad-speaker setup tuned by Waves. It delivers a wider soundstage than the MacBook, with excellent stereo imaging that makes movies feel immersive. However, the bass is not as deep, and at higher volumes, I noticed some distortion on bass-heavy tracks. The XPS also has a slight brightness to its sound that can make vocals sound a bit thin. For video conferencing, the 1080p webcam and dual-array mics are solid, but the audio capture isn't as natural as the MacBook's—there's a slight hollow quality to the sound.

The ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12 is the surprise of the group. Lenovo has upgraded its speaker system, and the result is a much more balanced sound than previous generations. It's not as powerful as the MacBook or as wide as the XPS, but it's perfectly adequate for everyday use. The real standout is the microphone array—ThinkPads have always excelled at voice capture, and this generation is no different. In noisy environments, the AI-based noise suppression does an impressive job of isolating your voice. If you live in Zoom meetings, the X1 Carbon is arguably the best choice of the three.

For creative professionals and media enthusiasts, the MacBook Pro is the clear winner. The XPS 16 is a great all-rounder with a slight edge in soundstage, and the ThinkPad is the conference call champion. But here's the thing: laptop speakers have improved so much that even budget models now sound decent. The real differentiator is how the audio integrates with the rest of the system—and Apple's seamless ecosystem gives it an edge.

Tablet Audio: iPad Pro M4 vs. Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra vs. Pixel Tablet

Tablets occupy a unique space in the audio world. They're big enough to deliver meaningful sound, but they're also used with headphones and external speakers. I tested each tablet's built-in speakers, as well as their Bluetooth audio performance with a pair of high-end wireless earbuds.

The iPad Pro M4 is, unsurprisingly, the audio leader. Apple has equipped it with a four-speaker system that adapts to the device's orientation, and the result is stunning. The sound is powerful, detailed, and has a genuine sense of space that makes watching movies on the iPad feel like a mini-cinema experience. Bass is surprisingly deep for a tablet, and the highs are silky smooth. The iPad also supports high-resolution audio formats, and when paired with quality headphones, it's a fantastic portable music player.

The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is a close second. Samsung has tuned its quad speakers with AKG, and they deliver a punchy, energetic sound that's great for music. The bass is slightly more pronounced than the iPad, but the overall balance is a bit less refined. The Tab also supports Dolby Atmos, which enhances the movie-watching experience. However, I found that at maximum volume, the sound becomes slightly harsh, and the speakers don't handle complex tracks as gracefully as the iPad.

The Pixel Tablet is a different beast. It's designed primarily as a smart display, and its audio reflects that. The built-in speakers are decent for casual listening and voice assistant interactions, but they lack the power and clarity of the iPad or Tab. The sound is a bit flat, and there's no real bass response. However, the Pixel Tablet's real audio strength lies in its ability to pair with Google's ecosystem—you can easily cast audio to other speakers, and the integration with Google Home is seamless.

If you're buying a tablet primarily for media consumption, the iPad Pro M4 is the obvious choice. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is a great alternative for Android users who want a more bass-forward sound. The Pixel Tablet is best suited for smart home use, not serious audio listening. And remember, for any tablet, a good pair of Bluetooth headphones will always outperform the built-in speakers—so factor that into your budget.

Matching Your Audio Needs to the Right Device

Now that we've broken down the audio performance of each device, let's talk about how to choose based on your specific needs. The truth is, there's no single "best" device—it all depends on how you use your gadgets.

If you're a music lover who listens primarily through your phone's speakers, the Galaxy S25 Ultra is your best bet. Its bass-heavy tuning makes tracks sound exciting, and the loud volume is perfect for sharing music with friends. If you're more into podcasts and audiobooks, the Pixel 9 Pro's clear, neutral sound will serve you better. And if you want a phone that does everything well, the iPhone 16 Pro Max is the all-rounder.

For laptop users, consider your primary use case. If you're a content creator or you spend hours watching movies on your laptop, the MacBook Pro's superior speakers are worth the investment. If you're a business professional who lives in video calls, the ThinkPad X1 Carbon's microphone quality is unmatched. The Dell XPS 16 is the best middle ground—it's great for media and work, with a slight edge in soundstage for movies.

Tablet buyers should think about how they'll use the device. The iPad Pro M4 is the ultimate media consumption tablet, with audio that rivals some portable speakers. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is perfect for Android users who want a powerful, bass-forward sound. And the Pixel Tablet is best for those who want a smart display that can also play music—not a dedicated audio device.

One more thing to consider: connectivity. All of these devices support Bluetooth 5.3 or later, but the codec support varies. Apple devices use AAC, which is excellent but proprietary. Samsung and Google support a range of codecs including aptX and LDAC, which can deliver higher-quality wireless audio if you have compatible headphones. If you're an audiophile, this might be the deciding factor.

The Verdict: Which Device Delivers the Best Audio Value?

After a month of intensive testing, I can confidently say that audio quality has become a major differentiator in the premium tech space. The days of tinny laptop speakers and muffled phone audio are long gone—today's flagship devices produce sound that would have been unthinkable just a few years ago.

In the smartphone category, the iPhone 16 Pro Max offers the best overall audio experience, but the Galaxy S25 Ultra is the value pick for bass lovers. The Pixel 9 Pro is the budget-friendly option that still delivers excellent clarity for spoken-word content. For laptops, the MacBook Pro is the undisputed king of audio, but the ThinkPad X1 Carbon offers the best value for business users who prioritize voice quality. The Dell XPS 16 is the best all-rounder, balancing media and work needs. And in tablets, the iPad Pro M4 is the premium choice, while the Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra offers comparable performance at a slightly lower price.

Here's my final advice: don't buy a device based on specs alone. Go to a store, play your favorite songs on each device, and listen carefully. Your ears are the best judge. And remember, the built-in speakers are just one part of the audio equation—a good pair of headphones or earbuds can elevate any of these devices to new heights. Invest in both, and you'll never settle for mediocre sound again.

Conclusion

Audio quality is no longer an afterthought in the tech world—it's a defining feature that can make or break your daily experience. The iPhone 16 Pro Max, MacBook Pro, and iPad Pro M4 lead their respective categories with exceptional sound, but Samsung, Google, Dell, and Lenovo are closing the gap with impressive offerings of their own. The key is to match the device to your specific audio needs, whether that's bass-heavy music, crystal-clear conference calls, or immersive movie watching. Take the time to listen before you buy, and you'll find a device that not only looks great on paper but sounds great in practice. Happy listening!
