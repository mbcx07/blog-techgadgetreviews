The Ultimate Audio Face-Off: Smartphones, Laptops, and Tablets Compared for Sound Enthusiasts

2026-07-01 · Audio · Tags: audio, The, techgadgetreviews

In the world of tech reviews, audio often plays second fiddle to display specs, processor speeds, and camera megapixels. But for many of us, sound is the soul of the device. Whether you're streaming lossless tracks, binging a cinematic series, or hopping on a critical video call, the audio quality of your phone, laptop, or tablet can make or break the experience. As a reviewer who spends hours with these gadgets, I've learned that specs on paper rarely tell the full story. Real-world audio performance involves speaker tuning, codec support, amplifier quality, and even the physical chassis design. In this comprehensive guide, I pit the most popular flagship smartphones, ultraportable laptops, and premium tablets against each other in a series of head-to-head audio tests. From the iPhone 16 Pro to the Galaxy S25 Ultra, from the MacBook Air M3 to the Dell XPS 13 Plus, and from the iPad Pro to the Galaxy Tab S9, we'll dive deep into what makes each device sing—or fall flat. Plus, I'll share a practical buying guide to help you choose the right audio-centric gadget for your lifestyle.

Smartphone Audio Showdown: iPhone 16 Pro vs Galaxy S25 Ultra vs Pixel 9 Pro

Smartphones are the most personal audio devices we own. They're our portable jukeboxes, podcast players, and hands-free communication hubs. When comparing the top three flagships—Apple's iPhone 16 Pro, Samsung's Galaxy S25 Ultra, and Google's Pixel 9 Pro—audio performance varies significantly despite similar price points.

The iPhone 16 Pro continues Apple's tradition of excellent built-in speakers. The stereo setup is tuned with a wider soundstage and slightly more bass presence than last year's model. During my listening tests, the iPhone handled complex tracks like Queen's "Bohemian Rhapsody" with impressive clarity, separating vocals and instruments without muddiness. The speaker grilles are placed on the bottom and the earpiece, creating a true stereo effect when held in landscape. However, the iPhone still lacks a 3.5mm headphone jack, and its Bluetooth codec support tops out at AAC, which is fine for Apple Music but not for high-res Android codecs like LDAC.

Samsung's Galaxy S25 Ultra takes a different approach. The dual speakers, tuned by AKG, are louder and more aggressive. They produce a punchier low-end, which is great for action movies but can sometimes overpower midrange frequencies. In side-by-side tests, the Galaxy's speakers felt more immersive for gaming and YouTube, but they lacked the refinement of the iPhone's output. On the wireless front, the S25 Ultra supports LDAC and aptX Adaptive, giving Android users access to higher bitrate streaming. It also retains a USB-C port for digital audio, but no headphone jack either.

Google's Pixel 9 Pro is the dark horse. Its speakers are surprisingly balanced, with a neutral frequency response that appeals to audiophiles who prefer accuracy over hype. The Pixel's audio codec support includes aptX and AAC, but not LDAC—a notable omission for a flagship in 2025. However, Google's software processing, including adaptive sound and spatial audio, does an excellent job of upscaling lower-quality sources. In real-world usage, the Pixel is the best for voice calls, with clear microphone pickup and noise reduction that makes the other two feel dated.

So which phone wins? It depends on your priorities. For raw loudness and bass, the Galaxy S25 Ultra takes the crown. For balanced, natural sound and seamless integration with Apple's ecosystem, the iPhone 16 Pro is unbeatable. And for pure clarity and call quality, the Pixel 9 Pro is a sleeper hit. My advice: if you're an Android user who values high-res wireless audio, go Samsung; if you're already in the Apple ecosystem, the iPhone is a no-brainer; and if you want a phone that just sounds right without fuss, the Pixel is your friend.

Laptop Audio Comparison: MacBook Air M3 vs Dell XPS 13 Plus vs ThinkPad X1 Nano Gen 4

Laptops are the workhorses of the tech world, but their audio capabilities are often an afterthought. Yet with more people working from home and consuming media on their laptops, speaker and headphone output matter more than ever. I spent a week with three ultraportable powerhouses: the MacBook Air M3, the Dell XPS 13 Plus, and the ThinkPad X1 Nano Gen 4.

The MacBook Air M3 is a revelation. Apple's custom silicon includes a dedicated audio processor that drives a four-speaker sound system with force-cancelling woofers. The result is a soundstage that feels far wider than the laptop's slim profile suggests. In my listening tests, the MacBook Air handled classical music with delicate highs and a surprisingly robust low end. It also supports high-impedance headphones through the 3.5mm jack, which has a built-in DAC that can drive 600-ohm cans with ease—something rare in ultraportables. The only downside is the lack of a USB-A port for legacy audio devices, but adapters are cheap.

Dell's XPS 13 Plus, on the other hand, has a more aggressive tuning. Its stereo speakers, placed on the sides of the keyboard, produce a forward, mid-focused sound that's excellent for podcasts and dialogue. However, they lack the depth and spatial imaging of the MacBook. The XPS also has a 3.5mm jack, but the audio output is noticeably weaker—it struggled to drive my Sennheiser HD 650 headphones without an external amp. On the wireless side, the XPS supports aptX and AAC, but no LDAC. For a productivity laptop, the XPS is fine, but it's not an audiophile's dream.

Lenovo's ThinkPad X1 Nano Gen 4 is the lightest of the trio, but its audio is surprisingly competent. The quad-speaker setup, tuned by Dolby Atmos, provides a balanced profile with a slight emphasis on vocals. It's not as loud as the MacBook, but it's more consistent across volume levels. The headphone jack is solid, with a clean signal and enough power for most IEMs. The ThinkPad also includes a far-field microphone array that makes video calls crystal clear—a feature that often goes unnoticed but is crucial for remote workers.

In the laptop arena, the MacBook Air M3 is the clear winner for audio quality. Its combination of hardware and software optimization is unmatched. The Dell XPS 13 Plus is a close second for those who prefer a warmer sound signature, but it falls short in headphone amplification. The ThinkPad X1 Nano Gen 4 is the best all-rounder if you need a lightweight laptop that handles both media and calls without breaking a sweat. If you're a music producer or a discerning listener, the MacBook is worth the extra cost.

Tablet Audio Comparison: iPad Pro vs Galaxy Tab S9 vs Pixel Tablet

Tablets have become the go-to devices for streaming and gaming, but their audio quality is often overlooked. I compared the latest iPad Pro (M4), the Samsung Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra, and the Google Pixel Tablet to see which one delivers the best sound experience.

The iPad Pro is a beast. Its four-speaker audio system automatically adjusts to the tablet's orientation, providing a consistent stereo image whether you hold it in portrait or landscape. The sound is rich, detailed, and remarkably loud for a tablet—it can easily fill a medium-sized room. The iPad Pro also supports spatial audio with dynamic head tracking when used with AirPods Pro, which is a game-changer for immersive movie watching. The headphone jack is absent, but the USB-C port supports high-res audio output. In my tests, the iPad Pro's speakers were the most impressive of the trio, with a wide frequency response and minimal distortion at high volumes.

Samsung's Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra takes a different approach. It has a quad-speaker system tuned by AKG, but the sound is more bass-heavy and less refined than the iPad. It's great for action movies and gaming, but acoustic music can sound a bit boomy. The Tab S9 Ultra also supports LDAC and aptX for wireless audio, giving it an edge over the iPad in terms of codec flexibility. However, the speakers lack the spatial processing that Apple offers. The Tab S9 Ultra is also heavier and harder to hold for long periods, which affects the listening experience.

Google's Pixel Tablet is a budget-friendly option that surprisingly holds its own. Its dual speakers are positioned on the sides, producing a clear, balanced sound that's perfect for podcasts and video calls. The Pixel Tablet also has a unique feature: it doubles as a smart display with a charging speaker dock. When docked, the sound quality improves dramatically, with a fuller bass response and better separation. However, when used as a standalone tablet, it's not in the same league as the iPad or Samsung. The Pixel Tablet is the best value for casual listeners, but it lacks the premium audio hardware of its competitors.

For pure audio performance, the iPad Pro is the undisputed king. Its speaker system is a marvel of engineering, and the spatial audio support is a killer feature. The Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra is a close second for Android users who want high-res wireless audio, but its speakers are less accurate. The Pixel Tablet is a great budget option, especially if you plan to use it primarily as a smart display. If you're a cinephile or an audiophile, the iPad Pro is worth every penny.

Real-World Audio Performance: What Actually Matters

Spec sheets can tell you about speaker wattage, driver sizes, and codec support, but they don't capture how a device sounds in real life. Over the years, I've developed a testing methodology that goes beyond numbers. Here's what I focus on when evaluating audio performance:

  • Frequency Response: I measure the output across the audible spectrum (20Hz–20kHz) using a calibrated microphone and software. A flat response is ideal, but many consumer devices add a "V-shaped" curve to boost bass and treble, which can be exciting but fatiguing.
  • Distortion: I push the volume to maximum and listen for crackling or muddiness. A good device should remain clean even at 80% volume.
  • Soundstage and Imaging: I play binaural recordings to see how well the device places instruments in space. A wide, precise soundstage enhances immersion.
  • Codec Support: For wireless audio, the codec determines the maximum bitrate. LDAC and aptX Adaptive are superior to AAC, but only if the source and sink both support them.
  • Headphone Output: For wired listening, I test the output impedance and power. A good headphone jack can drive high-impedance headphones without distortion.

In my experience, the devices that excel in these areas are the ones that invest in custom audio chips and tuned speaker enclosures. Apple, for instance, uses a dedicated audio DSP in its M-series chips, while Samsung collaborates with AKG. Google relies more on software processing, which can be hit or miss. When you're shopping for a new gadget, don't just look at the number of speakers—look at how they're implemented. A single well-designed speaker can outperform two poorly placed ones.

Buying Guide: How to Choose the Right Audio-Centric Device

With so many options, it's easy to get overwhelmed. Here's a practical guide to help you decide which device suits your audio needs best, based on your primary use case.

For the Commuter: If you're constantly on the go, your smartphone is your main audio device. Prioritize battery life, Bluetooth codec support, and speaker quality for hands-free calls. The Galaxy S25 Ultra is a great choice for Android users, while the iPhone 16 Pro is ideal for Apple users. If you're willing to carry a separate DAC, the Pixel 9 Pro's clean USB-C output is a hidden gem.

For the Remote Worker: Your laptop is your lifeline. Look for a device with excellent microphone array and speaker clarity for video conferencing. The MacBook Air M3 is the best all-around, but the ThinkPad X1 Nano Gen 4 is a close second if you need Windows and a lighter chassis. If you're on a budget, the Dell XPS 13 Plus is a solid choice, but you'll need an external USB DAC for serious headphone listening.

For the Home Streamer: A tablet is perfect for couch-side movie marathons. The iPad Pro's spatial audio is unmatched, but the Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra offers more codec flexibility. If you're a casual listener, the Pixel Tablet with its speaker dock is an unbeatable value. Just remember that the dock adds to the cost, but it transforms the tablet into a smart speaker when not in use.

For the Audiophile: If you demand the highest fidelity, you'll need a device that supports lossless audio and has a quality DAC. The MacBook Air M3's headphone jack is the best among laptops, and the iPad Pro's USB-C output is excellent. For smartphones, the Galaxy S25 Ultra's LDAC support is a must. However, for true audiophile performance, you might want to invest in a dedicated portable DAC/amp, which can elevate any device's output.

Ultimately, the best device is the one that fits your lifestyle and your ears. Don't be swayed by marketing buzzwords like "360 Reality Audio" or "Dolby Atmos"—always test with your own music. Most electronics stores have demo units, so bring your favorite tracks and give them a listen. Your ears will thank you.

Conclusion

Audio is a personal and often emotional experience, and the devices we choose to listen to can significantly impact our daily enjoyment. In this face-off, we've seen that no single device is perfect for everyone. The iPhone 16 Pro excels in speaker balance, the Galaxy S25 Ultra wins on loudness and codec support, and the Pixel 9 Pro shines in call quality. For laptops, the MacBook Air M3 is the gold standard, while the ThinkPad X1 Nano Gen 4 offers a compelling alternative for Windows users. And in the tablet world, the iPad Pro remains the king of sound, with the Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra and Pixel Tablet offering their own unique strengths.

When you're ready to make a purchase, remember to consider not just the hardware but also the ecosystem you're invested in. The best audio device is the one that integrates seamlessly with your existing gadgets and services. As technology evolves, we can expect even more impressive audio innovations, from adaptive sound profiles to AI-driven noise cancellation. Until then, happy listening, and may your playlists always sound amazing.
