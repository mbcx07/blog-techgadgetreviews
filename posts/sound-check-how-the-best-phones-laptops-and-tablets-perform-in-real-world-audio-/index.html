Sound Check: How the Best Phones, Laptops, and Tablets Perform in Real-World Audio Tests

2026-05-31 · Audio · Tags: techgadgetreviews, Sound, audio

In the world of tech, audio is often the unsung hero. We obsess over screen resolution, processor speeds, and camera megapixels, but the moment we press play on a favorite track or settle in for a movie, the quality of sound can make or break the experience. As a reviewer who has spent countless hours with the latest gadgets, I've learned that specs alone don't tell the full story. That's why I've put the most talked-about smartphones, laptops, and tablets through a rigorous battery of real-world audio tests—listening to music, podcasts, and video content across a variety of genres and environments. In this comprehensive comparison, I'll break down how the iPhone 16 Pro Max, Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra, Google Pixel 9 Pro, MacBook Pro, Dell XPS 13, ThinkPad X1 Carbon, and leading tablets like the iPad Pro and Galaxy Tab S9 handle sound. Whether you're a casual listener or a dedicated audiophile, this guide will help you understand which devices truly deliver—and which ones just look good on paper.

Smartphone Audio Showdown: iPhone vs. Galaxy vs. Pixel

Smartphones have become our primary music players, and the three flagship contenders—Apple, Samsung, and Google—each take a distinct approach to audio. The iPhone 16 Pro Max continues Apple's tradition of excellent built-in speakers, with a stereo setup that produces surprisingly deep bass and crisp highs. During my tests, the iPhone handled rock anthems with punchy drums and clear vocals, while classical pieces revealed subtle string textures that many phones muddy. The Galaxy S25 Ultra, on the other hand, boasts a tuned speaker system by AKG, and it shows. Its soundstage is wider, and the mids are more forward, making it ideal for podcasts and dialogue-heavy content. However, the Pixel 9 Pro, while not as loud as its rivals, offers a more neutral and accurate frequency response—a boon for purists who want to hear music as the artist intended.

When it comes to wired audio, the landscape is more complicated. None of these phones include a headphone jack, so you'll need a USB-C dongle or a Bluetooth pair. The iPhone supports AAC and has a solid internal DAC, but it lacks the high-resolution codecs like LDAC that Android phones offer. The Galaxy S25 Ultra and Pixel 9 Pro both support LDAC and aptX Adaptive, which can stream higher-quality audio over Bluetooth if you have compatible headphones. In my blind listening tests with a pair of wired IEMs via a USB-C dongle, the Pixel 9 Pro actually produced the cleanest signal, with the least amount of background hiss. The Galaxy was close behind, while the iPhone, though clean, had a slightly warmer tilt that some might find pleasing.

For most users, the built-in speakers are what matter day to day. Here, the Galaxy S25 Ultra takes the crown for sheer volume and clarity, but the iPhone's spatial audio feature—which simulates surround sound—makes it a better choice for watching movies without headphones. The Pixel, meanwhile, is the most balanced, but its lower maximum volume might disappoint in noisy environments. Ultimately, your choice depends on whether you prioritize loudness, accuracy, or immersive features.

Laptop Audio: MacBook Pro vs. Dell XPS vs. ThinkPad

Laptops are often used for work, but they're also our portable entertainment centers. The MacBook Pro has long been the gold standard for laptop audio, and the latest M3 model continues that legacy. Its six-speaker system with force-cancelling woofers produces a rich, room-filling sound that rivals many external speakers. In my tests, the MacBook handled bass-heavy electronic music without distortion, and the highs were never harsh. The Dell XPS 13, with its Waves MaxxAudio tuning, is also impressive, but it lacks the sheer power of the MacBook. The XPS's speakers are front-firing, which helps with clarity, but they can sound a bit thin at higher volumes. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon, known for its business pedigree, surprisingly holds its own. Its Dolby Atmos speaker system delivers a wide soundstage, and while it doesn't have the bass depth of the MacBook, it's more than adequate for conference calls and background music.

Headphone output is another critical factor. The MacBook Pro's 3.5mm jack is remarkably clean, with a high-quality DAC that can drive high-impedance headphones without breaking a sweat. The Dell XPS and ThinkPad both have decent jacks, but they're not in the same league. If you're an audiophile who plans to use wired headphones, the MacBook is the clear winner. However, if you rely on Bluetooth, all three support aptX and AAC, but the MacBook's Bluetooth implementation is slightly more stable in my experience.

One area where the XPS and ThinkPad shine is customization. Dell's MaxxAudio software allows you to tweak the EQ to your liking, and Lenovo's Dolby Access app offers presets for different content types. Apple, on the other hand, keeps things simple with a basic EQ in the Music app, but the hardware is so good that you rarely need to adjust anything. For a laptop that doubles as a portable speaker, the MacBook Pro is unbeatable. But if you're on a budget and need a Windows machine, the XPS 13 offers the best balance of audio quality and value.

Tablet Audio: iPad Pro vs. Galaxy Tab S9 vs. Surface Pro

Tablets have evolved into media consumption powerhouses, and their audio capabilities are a major selling point. The iPad Pro, with its four-speaker system, is a marvel. It automatically adjusts the output based on orientation, so you get the same immersive sound whether you're holding it in portrait or landscape. The bass is surprisingly deep for a tablet, and the stereo separation is excellent. The Galaxy Tab S9, with its quad speakers tuned by AKG, is equally impressive, offering a slightly brighter sound that's great for vocals. In my side-by-side tests, the iPad Pro had a more natural timbre, while the Tab S9 was more energetic. The Surface Pro, which I included for comparison, has a dual-speaker setup that's decent but not in the same league. It's fine for casual listening, but if you're serious about audio, you'll want to look elsewhere.

All three tablets support high-resolution audio over Bluetooth, but the iPad Pro and Galaxy Tab S9 also have excellent DACs for wired listening. The iPad Pro's USB-C port can output audio to external DACs, and it supports lossless audio on Apple Music. The Galaxy Tab S9 supports LDAC and has a headphone jack—a rarity in 2025—which is a huge plus for wired enthusiasts. The Surface Pro, unfortunately, lacks a headphone jack and its USB-C audio implementation is less refined.

For video streaming, the iPad Pro's spatial audio is a game-changer. It creates a virtual surround sound field that makes you feel like you're in a movie theater. The Galaxy Tab S9's Dolby Atmos support is also impressive, but it's not as seamless as Apple's implementation. If you're a movie buff, the iPad Pro is the clear winner. If you're a music lover who wants the flexibility of a headphone jack and LDAC, the Galaxy Tab S9 is the better choice. The Surface Pro, while a great productivity device, is an also-ran in the audio department.

The Audio Buying Guide: What to Look For

With so many options, how do you choose the right device for your audio needs? Start by considering your primary use case. If you're a commuter who listens to music on the go, a smartphone with good built-in speakers and strong Bluetooth codec support is essential. The Galaxy S25 Ultra and Pixel 9 Pro are excellent choices, but if you're deeply invested in the Apple ecosystem, the iPhone 16 Pro Max won't disappoint. For home use, a laptop with a powerful speaker system like the MacBook Pro can replace a dedicated Bluetooth speaker, saving you money and desk space. If you're a content creator who needs accurate monitoring, the MacBook's clean headphone output is a must.

Another key factor is connectivity. Do you still use wired headphones? If so, look for devices with a headphone jack or a high-quality USB-C DAC. The Galaxy Tab S9 and ThinkPad X1 Carbon are rare examples that still include the jack. For Bluetooth, check for support of aptX, LDAC, or AAC—these codecs determine the maximum audio quality over wireless. Also, consider the speaker configuration: more speakers generally mean better stereo separation, but placement and tuning matter just as much. Finally, don't overlook software. Some devices offer EQ presets or spatial audio features that can enhance your listening experience. The iPad Pro's spatial audio and the MacBook's built-in EQ are prime examples.

Value for money is also crucial. The MacBook Pro is expensive, but its audio performance justifies the price for professionals. The Dell XPS 13 offers 80% of the MacBook's audio quality at a lower cost. Similarly, the Pixel 9 Pro is more affordable than the iPhone or Galaxy, yet it delivers comparable audio quality. The key is to identify which features you can't live without and then find the device that offers them at the best price.

Real-World Testing Methodology

To ensure these comparisons are fair, I used a consistent testing protocol. I played a curated playlist of tracks spanning pop, rock, jazz, classical, and electronic music, as well as podcasts and movie clips. I listened to each device in a quiet room, a noisy café, and outdoors to assess volume and clarity in different environments. For wired tests, I used a high-quality USB-C dongle and a pair of reference IEMs. For Bluetooth, I paired each device with the same set of wireless headphones to eliminate variables. I also measured frequency response using a calibrated microphone and software, but I relied primarily on subjective listening because that's what matters in real life. Each device was tested at default settings, with no EQ adjustments, to reflect out-of-the-box performance.

My findings were consistent across multiple sessions. The MacBook Pro and iPad Pro consistently delivered the most accurate and immersive sound, while the Galaxy S25 Ultra and Tab S9 excelled in loudness and energy. The Pixel 9 Pro and Dell XPS 13 were the dark horses, offering excellent value without sacrificing quality. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon and Surface Pro, while competent, didn't stand out in any audio category. These results align with the technical specs, but they also reveal nuances that specs can't capture—like how the iPhone's spatial audio makes movies feel more cinematic, or how the Galaxy's AKG tuning makes vocals pop.

Conclusion

After weeks of testing, one thing is clear: audio quality is no longer an afterthought in flagship devices. The iPhone 16 Pro Max, Galaxy S25 Ultra, and Pixel 9 Pro each offer compelling audio experiences, but they cater to different preferences. The MacBook Pro remains the undisputed king of laptop audio, while the iPad Pro and Galaxy Tab S9 are the best tablets for sound. For most people, the choice comes down to ecosystem and budget. If you're an Apple user, the seamless integration of spatial audio across iPhone, MacBook, and iPad is a huge advantage. If you prefer Android and Windows, the Galaxy and Dell devices offer comparable performance at a lower price. Ultimately, the best device is the one that makes your music sound the way you want it to. So, before you buy, take a listen—your ears will thank you.
