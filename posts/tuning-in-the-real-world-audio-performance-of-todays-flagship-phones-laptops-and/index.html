Tuning In: The Real-World Audio Performance of Today's Flagship Phones, Laptops, and Tablets

2026-05-14 · Audio · Tags: techgadgetreviews, Tuning, audio

In the world of tech, we often obsess over processor speeds, camera megapixels, and display resolutions. But for many of us, the most intimate and daily interaction with our devices is through sound—whether it's a favorite playlist, a podcast, a movie, or a video call. Audio quality can make or break the experience, yet it's frequently an afterthought in spec sheets. As a reviewer who spends hours with every device, I've learned that the numbers on paper rarely tell the full story. That's why I've spent the last month testing the latest flagship smartphones, laptops, and tablets with a critical ear, focusing on what actually matters: real-world audio performance, codec support, speaker clarity, and headphone output. Here's how the top contenders stack up for anyone who takes their sound seriously.

Smartphone Showdown: iPhone 16 Pro Max vs. Galaxy S25 Ultra vs. Pixel 9 Pro

Smartphones are the most personal audio devices we own. They're our portable jukeboxes, podcast players, and often our primary source for streaming. The three biggest players—Apple, Samsung, and Google—each take a different approach to audio, and the differences are more pronounced than you might think.

The iPhone 16 Pro Max continues Apple's tradition of clean, balanced sound. Its stereo speakers are impressively loud and well-tuned, with a surprising amount of bass for a phone. The midrange is clear, and vocals sound natural, making it excellent for podcasts and audiobooks. But the real star is the headphone output via the Lightning-to-3.5mm adapter (or USB-C now). Apple's DAC is transparent, with no coloration, and it drives high-impedance headphones better than most phones. However, the lack of a headphone jack and the reliance on dongles is a minor annoyance. Bluetooth support is solid, with AAC and LDAC (though LDAC is not officially supported on iOS, it works via third-party apps).

Samsung's Galaxy S25 Ultra takes a different route. It still includes a headphone jack—a rarity in 2025—and that's a huge win for audiophiles. The built-in DAC is surprisingly good, with a warm, slightly colored sound that adds a touch of richness to music. The speakers are also excellent, with a wider soundstage than the iPhone, but they can get a bit harsh at maximum volume. Samsung also supports a wide range of Bluetooth codecs, including aptX Adaptive and LDAC, giving you more flexibility with wireless headphones. If you're a wired headphone fan, the S25 Ultra is the clear winner.

Google's Pixel 9 Pro is the dark horse. It lacks a headphone jack, but its USB-C audio implementation is clean and neutral. The speakers are the most balanced of the three, with a focus on clarity rather than bass. The Pixel's real strength is in its software—Google's Adaptive Sound feature uses the microphone to tune the speakers to your environment, which genuinely improves the listening experience. For Bluetooth, it supports aptX and LDAC, but the implementation is a bit finicky. Overall, the Pixel is the best for those who prioritize accuracy over punch.

Laptop Audio: MacBook Pro vs. Dell XPS 15 vs. ThinkPad X1 Carbon

Laptops are often used for media consumption, video editing, and conference calls, so audio quality matters more than ever. The MacBook Pro has long been the gold standard, but Dell and Lenovo have been catching up.

The MacBook Pro (14-inch, M3 Pro) delivers the best laptop speakers I've ever heard. The six-speaker system with force-cancelling woofers produces deep, tight bass and a wide soundstage that rivals some external speakers. It's genuinely impressive for a laptop. The headphone jack is also top-tier, with a high-impedance mode that can drive professional headphones. Apple's audio software, including the built-in equalizer and spatial audio support, adds to the experience. The only downside is the price—you're paying a premium for that audio quality.

The Dell XPS 15 (2025) is a close second. Its speakers are also excellent, with a slightly brighter tuning that makes vocals stand out. The bass is not as deep as the MacBook, but it's still satisfying. The headphone jack is clean, but it lacks the high-impedance support, so you'll need an external amp for power-hungry cans. Dell's audio software, Waves MaxxAudio, offers a lot of customization, but it can also introduce artifacts if you're not careful. For the price, the XPS is a great value, especially if you're already in the Windows ecosystem.

The ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12 is a business laptop first, but its audio is surprisingly good. The quad-speaker setup is clear and loud, but it lacks the bass response of the MacBook or XPS. The headphone jack is adequate, but nothing special. Lenovo's audio software is minimal, which is fine for most users. The X1 Carbon's real strength is its durability and keyboard, not its audio. If you're a professional who needs a reliable machine for calls and occasional music, it's fine, but audiophiles will want to look elsewhere.

Tablet Audio: iPad Pro vs. Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra vs. Surface Pro 10

Tablets are increasingly used for streaming and gaming, and their speakers are often the primary output. The iPad Pro has been the benchmark, but Samsung and Microsoft are making strides.

The iPad Pro (12.9-inch, M4) features a four-speaker system that automatically adjusts to the orientation. It's loud, clear, and has a surprising amount of bass for a tablet. The soundstage is wide, making it great for movies. The headphone jack is absent, but the USB-C audio is clean. Apple's spatial audio with dynamic head tracking works brilliantly on the iPad, especially with AirPods Pro. For media consumption, the iPad Pro is unmatched.

The Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra takes a different approach. It has four speakers tuned by AKG, and they're excellent—perhaps even better than the iPad in terms of sheer volume and clarity. The bass is punchy, and the highs are crisp. Samsung also includes a headphone jack, which is a rarity in tablets. The built-in DAC is good, but not as refined as the iPhone's. The Tab S9 Ultra also supports Dolby Atmos, which enhances the movie-watching experience. If you're an Android user, this is the tablet to get for audio.

The Surface Pro 10 is a hybrid, but its audio is often overlooked. The dual speakers are decent, but they lack the depth and richness of the iPad or Tab. The headphone jack is present, but the audio output is average. Microsoft's focus is on productivity, not entertainment, so the audio is serviceable but not exceptional. For a business tablet, it's fine, but for music lovers, it's a compromise.

Real-World Performance: What Actually Matters

Specs like speaker wattage and codec support are important, but real-world performance depends on how you use the device. I tested all these devices with a mix of streaming services (Spotify, Tidal, Apple Music), local FLAC files, and Bluetooth headphones (Sony WH-1000XM5, AirPods Pro 2, and a wired pair of Sennheiser HD 600). Here are my key takeaways:

  • Wired vs. wireless: If you're a purist, wired is still better. The Galaxy S25 Ultra and Tab S9 Ultra are the only ones with headphone jacks, but the iPhone and MacBook Pro have excellent DACs via USB-C. The Pixel and XPS are fine, but not exceptional.
  • Bluetooth codecs: LDAC and aptX Adaptive offer higher bitrates, but only if your headphones support them. Samsung and Google phones support these, while Apple sticks to AAC. In practice, AAC is very good, but LDAC can be better with high-res files.
  • Speaker quality: For casual listening, the MacBook Pro and iPad Pro are the best. The Galaxy S25 Ultra and Tab S9 Ultra are close, but they can sound a bit harsh at max volume. The iPhone and Pixel are more balanced.
  • Software tuning: Google's Adaptive Sound and Apple's spatial audio are game-changers. They genuinely improve the experience, while Samsung's and Dell's software can be hit-or-miss.

Buying Guide: How to Choose Based on Your Audio Needs

Now that you know how these devices perform, how do you decide? It comes down to your priorities and budget.

If you're a smartphone user who primarily listens to music on the go and values a headphone jack, the Galaxy S25 Ultra is the obvious choice. It offers the best wired audio experience and excellent wireless support. If you're invested in the Apple ecosystem and want the best speakers and spatial audio, the iPhone 16 Pro Max is worth the premium. The Pixel 9 Pro is the best value for those who want a balanced, clean sound without breaking the bank.

For laptops, the MacBook Pro is the audiophile's dream, but it's expensive. If you're on a budget, the Dell XPS 15 offers 90% of the audio quality at a lower price. The ThinkPad X1 Carbon is best for business users who need a reliable machine with decent audio, not for critical listening.

For tablets, the iPad Pro is the king of media consumption, but the Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra is a close second and offers a headphone jack. The Surface Pro 10 is only worth it if you need a Windows tablet for work and don't care about audio.

Conclusion

Audio quality is a deeply personal aspect of technology, and there's no one-size-fits-all answer. After weeks of testing, I've found that the best device for you depends on how you listen. If you're a wired purist, the Galaxy S25 Ultra and Tab S9 Ultra are your best bets. If you want the most immersive wireless experience, the iPhone and MacBook Pro with spatial audio are unbeatable. And if you're looking for value, the Pixel 9 Pro and Dell XPS 15 offer excellent performance without the premium price tag. No matter which you choose, remember that the best audio is the one you enjoy most. So tune in, listen closely, and pick the device that makes your music come alive.
