The Sound of 2025: How the Latest Phones, Laptops, and Tablets Stack Up for Audio Enthusiasts

2026-08-16 · Audio · Tags: audio, The, techgadgetreviews

When was the last time you truly listened to your devices? Not just heard them, but listened. In the rush to compare megapixels, processor speeds, and battery life, audio quality often gets relegated to a footnote. But think about it: every notification, every podcast, every video call, every song runs through your device's speakers or headphone output. The audio experience is the one thing you interact with constantly, whether you're a casual listener or a dedicated audiophile. This year's flagship phones, laptops, and tablets have made significant strides in audio technology, and I've spent the past month putting them through real-world listening tests to find out which ones actually deliver. From crowded coffee shops to quiet home offices, I've pushed these devices to their limits to give you the definitive audio verdict.

Smartphone Audio: iPhone 16 Pro Max vs Galaxy S25 Ultra vs Pixel 9 Pro

The smartphone is the device most people use for audio daily, so it's the natural starting point. I tested all three flagships with a mix of streaming music, lossless files, podcasts, and phone calls in various environments—from a noisy subway platform to a silent studio.

The iPhone 16 Pro Max continues Apple's tradition of excellent built-in speakers. The dual speaker setup produces a surprisingly wide soundstage for a phone, with clear highs and a noticeable bass response that doesn't distort at maximum volume. Apple's spatial audio implementation is the most refined in the industry, and when you're watching a movie or listening to a Dolby Atmos mix, the immersion is genuinely impressive. However, the lack of a headphone jack means you're either using Bluetooth or a dongle, and while AAC over Bluetooth is solid, it's not lossless. For audiophiles who insist on wired connections, this is a persistent frustration.

The Galaxy S25 Ultra takes a different approach. Samsung has tuned its speakers with AKG, and the result is a punchier, more aggressive sound profile. Bass is more pronounced, which makes it great for hip-hop and electronic music, but it can feel slightly muddy on acoustic tracks. Dolby Atmos support is excellent, and Samsung's implementation of Bluetooth codecs includes aptX and LDAC support, which is a significant advantage if you have high-end wireless headphones. In my testing, the Galaxy produced the loudest output of the three, but that volume came at the cost of some subtlety in the midrange.

The Pixel 9 Pro is the sleeper hit in this comparison. Google's speakers are tuned for clarity rather than volume, and the result is a more neutral, honest sound. It's not as exciting as the Galaxy's bass-heavy profile, but it's more accurate. The Pixel also supports LDAC and aptX, and Google's audio processing is particularly good at handling voice calls—the clearest of the three in noisy environments. I found myself reaching for the Pixel when I needed to take an important call in a busy area, and it never let me down.

For most people, the iPhone is the best all-rounder, but if you're an Android user who values codec flexibility, the Galaxy S25 Ultra is the better choice. The Pixel 9 Pro is for those who want accuracy over excitement.

Laptop Audio: MacBook Pro vs Dell XPS vs ThinkPad X1 Carbon

Laptops are where audio quality really matters, because they're often used for video calls, media consumption, and even music production. I tested the 14-inch MacBook Pro (M4), the Dell XPS 14, and the Lenovo ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12 over a two-week period, using everything from Spotify streams to high-bitrate FLAC files and Zoom meetings.

The MacBook Pro is the undisputed king of laptop audio. Apple's six-speaker system with force-cancelling woofers is not a gimmick—it genuinely sounds like a small soundbar. The bass response is deep and controlled, the mids are clear, and the highs are crisp without being harsh. Spatial audio on the MacBook Pro is also excellent, and it's the only laptop I've tested where I didn't immediately reach for external speakers. For video calls, the microphone array is equally impressive, picking up your voice clearly while rejecting background noise. In a test with a fan running in the background, the MacBook's mic isolated my voice perfectly.

The Dell XPS 14 is a close second. Its quad-speaker setup is well-tuned, with a slightly warmer profile than the MacBook. It's great for music and movies, but it lacks the MacBook's bass depth and spatial imaging. The XPS also has a solid microphone array, though it's not quite as good at noise rejection as Apple's. Where the XPS shines is in its flexibility—it supports a wider range of Bluetooth codecs, including aptX, which is a plus for Windows users with high-end wireless headphones.

The ThinkPad X1 Carbon is the weakest of the three for audio, which is expected for a business-focused laptop. The speakers are adequate for video calls and occasional media, but they lack the richness and depth of the MacBook or XPS. The microphone is excellent, though—it's the best of the three for conference calls, which is what ThinkPad users care about most. In a simulated conference call with multiple voices, the ThinkPad's mic array handled the mix with remarkable clarity.

If audio is a priority, the MacBook Pro is the clear winner. The XPS is a good alternative if you prefer Windows, and the ThinkPad is for those who prioritize call quality over media consumption.

Tablet Audio: iPad Pro vs Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra vs Pixel Tablet

Tablets are increasingly used for media consumption, so audio quality is a key differentiator. I tested the iPad Pro 13-inch (M4), the Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra, and the Pixel Tablet with a mix of streaming video, music, and gaming.

The iPad Pro's four-speaker system is remarkable. It automatically adjusts the output based on orientation, so whether you're holding it in portrait or landscape, the stereo image stays correct. The sound is detailed and well-balanced, with a surprising amount of bass for a device this thin. It's the best tablet audio I've ever heard, period. Watching a Dolby Atmos movie on the iPad Pro is a genuinely cinematic experience, and the speakers don't distort even at maximum volume.

The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra comes close, with its quad-speaker setup tuned by AKG. It's louder than the iPad Pro and has a slightly more aggressive sound profile, which some people will prefer. Dolby Atmos support is excellent, and it handles movie soundtracks with real authority. However, it's not quite as refined as the iPad Pro—the highs can get slightly harsh at maximum volume, and the bass, while punchy, lacks the iPad's control.

The Pixel Tablet is a different beast. It's designed primarily as a home hub, and its audio reflects that. The speakers are decent for a tablet, but they lack the clarity and depth of the iPad Pro or Galaxy Tab. The bass is weak, and the highs can sound tinny. It's fine for casual listening, but it's not a media powerhouse. If you're considering the Pixel Tablet for its audio, you'll be disappointed.

For media consumption, the iPad Pro is the best tablet you can buy. The Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is a strong alternative, especially if you prefer Android, and the Pixel Tablet is best for those who want a smart display that also happens to be a tablet.

The Buying Guide: Matching Devices to Your Audio Needs

Now that we've covered the individual devices, let's talk about how to build the right audio ecosystem for your needs. The key is to think about how you actually use audio in your daily life, not just what looks good on a spec sheet.

For the music producer or audio professional, the MacBook Pro is non-negotiable. Its combination of accurate speakers, low-latency audio processing, and excellent headphone output makes it the best tool for the job. Pair it with the iPad Pro as a secondary display or portable sketchpad, and you have a powerful mobile studio. For your phone, the iPhone 16 Pro Max is the natural choice, as it integrates seamlessly with Apple's ecosystem and offers the most accurate audio playback. The combination of these three devices gives you a closed-loop workflow that's hard to beat.

For the casual listener who wants great sound without breaking the bank, the Pixel 9 Pro is the best value phone for audio, offering excellent clarity and codec support at a lower price than the iPhone or Galaxy. Pair it with the Dell XPS 14 for your laptop, which offers great audio for less than the MacBook. For a tablet, the Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra is a great choice if you can find it on sale, but the standard Galaxy Tab S10 is also excellent and more affordable. This setup gives you flexibility across Android and Windows, with the ability to use high-quality Bluetooth codecs on all your devices.

For the video call warrior who spends their days in meetings, the ThinkPad X1 Carbon is the best laptop for call quality, and the Pixel 9 Pro is the best phone for voice clarity. For a tablet, the iPad Pro's microphone array is excellent, but you might not need a tablet at all if you're primarily working from a laptop. Focus your budget on a good external microphone and a pair of noise-cancelling headphones instead—that will make a bigger difference than any built-in speaker system.

For the movie and TV buff, the iPad Pro is the best tablet for media consumption, and the MacBook Pro is the best laptop. For your phone, the Galaxy S25 Ultra's punchy audio and Dolby Atmos support make it the best choice for watching content on the go. The combination of the iPad Pro and Galaxy S25 Ultra gives you two screens with excellent audio, whether you're at home or traveling.

Conclusion

Audio quality is not a luxury—it's a fundamental part of how we interact with our devices. This year's flagship phones, laptops, and tablets have made significant strides, and there's a perfect setup for every type of user. The MacBook Pro and iPad Pro are the gold standards for audio, but the Galaxy S25 Ultra and Dell XPS offer excellent alternatives for Android and Windows users. The Pixel 9 Pro is the best value for audio-focused buyers, and the ThinkPad X1 Carbon remains the king of conference calls. Whatever your needs, the key is to prioritize audio as much as you prioritize processing power and camera quality. Your ears will thank you.
